## Abstract <a href="#section-003" id="section-003"></a>

For tasks involving entity relationships, temporal change, local complexity, and continual feedback, this paper studies how to choose representation capacity, solving methods, and learning actions under limited observations and computational budgets. We propose the SGD-Net model family: typed graphs/high-dimensional Euclidean-equivariant messages describe spatial relationships; state-space models maintain causal memory; dynamic trees manage local representations, experts, or candidates; and pre-observation predictions paired with later outcomes form traceable error evidence. The system separates event-state updates, within-task refinement, window-level continual learning, and intergenerational consolidation. Candidate changes migrate per-layer states, parameters, optimizers, and execution layouts, and are automatically accepted as persistent versions after independent evaluation and applicable constraint checks.

The paper separately defines explicit temporal recurrence, implicit-equation forward passes and adjoint gradients, Lyapunov/dissipativity checks, reflection and retrospective analysis, and function/operator-space error analysis. It presents equivariance, contraction-mapping residual bounds, and representation-transfer properties for specific configurations, making clear that these do not establish unconditional stability or convergence of the whole network. The validation plan covers small mathematical tasks, world models, physical AI, molecular/cellular/materials tasks, and neuroscience, with equal-budget baselines, forgetting on old tasks, and end-to-end resource evaluation. The candidate contribution is an evidence-driven mechanism coordinating representation, computation, and learning; novelty, performance benefits, and general intelligence remain unproven.

**Keywords**: state-space models; E(n)-equivariant graphs; implicit solving; dynamic experts; continual learning; error estimation; reflection and retrospective analysis; hardware/software co-design.

## 1. Introduction <a href="#section-004" id="section-004"></a>

### 1.1 Background <a href="#section-005" id="section-005"></a>

Transformers have accelerated the development of large language and multimodal models, but global attention faces an $$O(N^2)$$ complexity bottleneck for long sequences, and generation often relies on autoregressive sampling in high-dimensional continuous probability spaces. Meanwhile, although neural solvers in scientific computing can fit complex functions, they still struggle to offer the stability, error bounds, and mesh adaptivity emphasized by conventional finite-element methods for strongly nonlinear problems, shocks, crack tips, and coupled multiphysics.

Recent state-space models, including SSM/Mamba, demonstrate the potential to replace explicit attention over the full history with latent-state evolution. Graph neural networks naturally address topological relationships, geometric neighborhoods, and knowledge-graph reasoning, while differentiable decision trees and deep forests provide discrete boundaries, conditional divide-and-conquer computation, and interpretable routing. This paper proposes that unifying these three mechanisms within a variational and stability framework may yield an architectural approach beyond increasing parameter counts in static large models.

### 1.2 Problem definition <a href="#section-006" id="section-006"></a>

The paper asks:

> Can a neural architecture provide local error estimation, adaptive structural refinement, and stability constraints analogous to finite-element/boundary-element methods while retaining modern neural networks' abilities in high-dimensional representation and learning from large datasets?

SGD-Net is proposed to investigate this question.

### 1.3 Candidate contributions and testable hypotheses <a href="#section-007" id="section-007"></a>

1. Define prediction–observation feedback with provenance, clocks, and versions, and use it as evidence for candidate changes to observations, representations, training, or solving.
2. Connect local tree/graph/state evolution with parameter, optimizer, and layout migration and subsequent competition, studying tradeoffs among quality, cost, and forgetting.
3. Specify interfaces and failure conditions among high-dimensional equivariance, implicit computation, applicable stability mechanisms, and reflection within one model family.
4. Establish ablations and numerical evaluations around actual task capabilities, without requiring full simulation of existing AI models' computations.

These are research proposals. Combining components or using high-dimensional equivariance is not itself claimed as established originality; distinctive additions require comparison against existing methods and experiments.

## 2. Related work <a href="#section-008" id="section-008"></a>

### 2.1 Transformers and explicit global attention <a href="#section-009" id="section-009"></a>

Transformer attention gains strong modeling capacity by explicitly computing pairwise token relationships, at the cost of rapidly increasing computation and storage with sequence length. Explicit global attention can become a bottleneck for long text, video, physical trajectories, and continual online interaction.

### 2.2 State-space models <a href="#section-010" id="section-010"></a>

A state-space model is commonly written as:

$$
\dot{h}(t)=Ah(t)+Bx(t), \qquad y(t)=Ch(t)+Dx(t)
$$

Its main advantage is compressing historical information into a latent state $$h(t)$$ and supporting long-range recurrence through stable discretization. Selective SSMs such as Mamba further make matrix parameters input-dependent, increasing expressiveness while retaining linear complexity.

### 2.3 Graph neural networks and physics-informed modeling <a href="#section-011" id="section-011"></a>

GNNs model non-Euclidean structures through nodes, edges, and message passing, supporting molecular graphs, transport networks, knowledge graphs, finite-element meshes, and complex systems. Physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) incorporate PDE residuals into neural-network losses, but convergence and stability remain challenging for strongly nonlinear and multiscale problems.

### 2.4 Differentiable decision trees and dynamic-structure models <a href="#section-012" id="section-012"></a>

Differentiable decision trees use soft gating or differentiable splits for conditional routing, offering interpretability, locality, and divide-and-conquer behavior. Compared with fixed-width neural networks, dynamic trees are better suited to adding expressive capacity in locally complex regions.

### 2.5 Adaptive finite elements and a posteriori error estimation <a href="#section-013" id="section-013"></a>

Adaptive finite-element methods identify high-residual elements through a posteriori error estimation and perform $$h$$-refinement, $$p$$-enrichment, or combined $$hp$$-refinement. Their central idea is to concentrate computation where errors are largest rather than distribute it uniformly throughout the domain.

### 2.6 Equivariant graph neural networks and geometric deep learning <a href="#section-014" id="section-014"></a>

Molecules, proteins, crystals, and physical particle fields commonly exhibit rotational, translational, or reflection symmetries. EGNN, SE(3) Transformer, Tensor Field Networks, NequIP, and MACE show that explicitly preserving E(n)/SE(3) equivariance in geometric tasks can reduce unnecessary sample complexity and improve physical consistency. SGD-Net uses equivariant GNNs as a scientific-geometric extension of ordinary topological GNNs and additionally requires invariant scalar residuals to drive dynamic-tree grow/shrink decisions.

### 2.7 Physical-conservation models and runtime safety wrappers <a href="#section-015" id="section-015"></a>

PINNs, Hamiltonian Neural Networks, Lagrangian Neural Networks, and conservative neural operators incorporate physical constraints into training objectives or architectures. However, training-time constraints alone cannot guarantee runtime safety in automated experimentation, industrial control, or medical decision support. SGD-Harness places conservation, geometric validity, equivariance consistency, and safety auditing after model outputs, forming an auditable runtime decision layer.

### 2.8 Crystal graphs, periodic boundaries, and materials learning <a href="#section-016" id="section-016"></a>

Materials and crystal modeling must handle unit cells, fractional coordinates, periodic boundary conditions, space-group symmetry, defect masks, and grain-boundary structures. CGCNN, MEGNet, M3GNet, CHGNet, and ALIGNN demonstrate the effectiveness of crystal-graph learning. SGD-Net uses `CrystalGraphBuilder` as an input layer for materials tasks and records adjacency, PBC offsets, and equivariance residuals in GeometryTrace.

### 2.9 Active learning, Bayesian optimization, and PIEVO-like scientific discovery <a href="#section-017" id="section-017"></a>

Bayesian optimization, Gaussian processes, information-directed sampling, and active learning are widely used in low-data scientific discovery. PIEVO-like frameworks further emphasize evolution of principle spaces, surprise-driven anomalies, and dual-loop search. SGD-Net does not replace GP/BO; it provides structured hypothesis graphs, dynamic-tree splitting, retrospective analysis of failures, and Harness review. BICU is a possible future hardware or CModel direction for accelerating covariance and acquisition-function computation.

### 2.10 AI accelerators and hardware for structural dynamic sparsity <a href="#section-018" id="section-018"></a>

Mainstream GPUs/TPUs/NPUs are better suited to dense GEMM and regular tensor computation, while key SGD-Net workloads include SSM scans, sparse topological message passing, dynamic-tree pruning, geometric neighborhood construction, Harness checks, and small Bayesian matrix computations. Hardware discussion is therefore limited to opportunity assessment at the trace/CModel stage; performance projections for systems not yet fabricated are not treated as measurements.

### 2.11 World models, hybrid solving, and model merging <a href="#section-019" id="section-019"></a>

Joint-embedding prediction approaches such as I-JEPA, V-JEPA, and V-JEPA 2 indicate that predicting abstract representations instead of reconstructing high-entropy pixels/tokens is an important direction for world models. However, physical and causal benchmarks such as IntPhys, Minimal Video Pairs, and CausalVQA also indicate substantial gaps between current video/multimodal world models and human-level physical reasoning. SGD-Net therefore treats JEPA as an external world-representation frontend, not a completed AGI core.

Complex scientific-intelligence systems also cannot rely solely on BP/SGD. Manifold parameters may use Riemannian Adam; dynamic-tree actions, PPO/Actor-Critic; non-differentiable hardware/experimental policies, ES/BO; constrained and sparse problems, Prox/ADMM; online states, EKF/UKF/Particle Filters; and edge-side experience feedback, methods inspired by FedAvg, Model Soups, Task Arithmetic, and TIES-Merging. These are extended experiments and future work, requiring reproducible evidence through `SolverTrace`, `AdaptationTrace`, and `ModelMergeTrace`.

## 3. Constructing SGD-Net models <a href="#section-020" id="section-020"></a>

### 3.1 Hierarchy and state definitions <a href="#section-021" id="section-021"></a>

A model instance comprises nine groups: A input contracts, B encoding, C graphs and geometry, D spatiotemporal blocks, E task heads, F bounded adaptation, G learning, H runtime, and L stability. Each A–H group contains 8 units and L contains 8, totaling 72. Training and runtime responsibilities in G/H are not automatically counted as neural layers.

Each layer distinguishes scalars h_i∈R^d, temporal states z_i∈R^s, vectors v_i∈R^{q×n}, and computational coordinates ξ_i∈R^n. Measured coordinates x retain their original records. Physical time t, solver iteration k, generation time τ, and parameter version ν are tracked separately. Static cellular or materials tasks can disable temporal state; expert, implicit, and manifold paths are enabled according to task evidence.

### 3.2 Graph messages and E(n) geometry <a href="#section-022" id="section-022"></a>

$$
r_{ij}=\xi_i-\xi_j,\quad m_{ij}=\phi_m(h_i,h_j,\|r_{ij}\|^2,e_{ij},c),
\quad\bar m_i=\operatorname{Agg}_{j\in\mathcal N(i)}m_{ij},
$$
$$
\xi_i^+=\xi_i+\eta\sum_j\phi_x(m_{ij})r_{ij}.
$$

φ_x outputs scalars, and n≥3 is configurable by task. Under the group action x→Qx+b, distances are invariant and r→Qr; the module is E(n)-equivariant when graph construction, conditioning, routing, and other paths are compatible. The original EGNN paper already supports high-dimensional Euclidean spaces, so this project cannot list 3→n alone as an innovation. Chiral tasks may use SE(3) or other appropriate representations. General manifolds additionally require metrics and tangent spaces, which are not determined automatically by n.

Periodic crystals use r_ij,k=H(s_i−s_j+k) while retaining the cell and shifts. Componentwise rounding alone cannot guarantee the nearest image for an arbitrary skew cell. Quadrature-field messages must retain volume weights; ordinary mean aggregation is not automatically an integral discretization.

### 3.3 SSMs and actual observation innovations <a href="#section-023" id="section-023"></a>

An explicit reference configuration consistent with document 36 is

$$
u_i=W_u[h_i,\bar m_i,c],\quad a=-\operatorname{softplus}(\alpha),\quad
\delta_i=\Delta t_i\operatorname{softplus}(W_\delta u_i+b_\delta),\quad
\bar a_i=\exp(\delta_i\odot a),
$$
$$
z_i^-=\bar a_i\odot z_i^{old}+(1-\bar a_i)\odot\phi_b(u_i),\qquad
z_i=z_i^-+\gamma_i\odot W_r\operatorname{Scale}(M_i(y_i-\hat y_i^-)).
$$

u/z/a/δ are s-dimensional, and γ is exactly zero when all observations are missing. Pre-observation predictions are stored by entity, physical time, and version. Later y participates only in correction and error evaluation; the same event must not decay again at each solver iteration. Scalar memory receives invariant innovations, while vector observations require compatible projections. This path updates memory; it is not equivalent to parameter learning, a complete Mamba model, or implicit numerical solving.

### 3.4 Actual implicit layers and gradients <a href="#section-024" id="section-024"></a>

IM01–IM06 handle assembly, initialization, solving, diagnostics, backpropagation, and acceptance. DEQ uses R(ζ)=ζ−fθ(ζ,u,G)=0, while implicit physical stepping uses R(x⁺)=x⁺−x−Δt fθ(x⁺,a,c)=0. Optimization layers specify separate objectives/constraints. Freeze parameters, graphs, and real memory throughout one solve and its backward pass, recording initialization, tolerances, bounds, stagnation, and timeouts.

When the residual is differentiable, the local root is unique, J_R is invertible, and other required conditions hold,

$$
J_R^T\lambda=\nabla_\zeta\mathcal L,\qquad
\nabla_\theta\mathcal L=\partial_\theta\mathcal L-(\partial_\theta R)^T\lambda.
$$

JVP/VJP operations can avoid explicitly forming a large Jacobian, but forward and backward solves still incur costs. Approximate-root and linear-solve errors affect gradients; truncated unrolling is a separate training path. Failure may return to an already checked explicit candidate or to unknown status; fallback correctness must not be assumed.

### 3.5 Dynamic trees, state migration, and continual learning <a href="#section-025" id="section-025"></a>

A representation tree refines computational representations, an expert tree adds local models, and a candidate tree manages search copies. Persistent growth is not triggered solely by one instance of η>ε: it requires persistent errors, quality checks, and evidence supporting insufficient representation. An expert mixture can be written as

$$
\hat y=\sum_{\ell}\pi_\ell(h,c)f_\ell(h,c),\quad \pi_\ell\geq0,\quad\sum_\ell\pi_\ell=1.
$$

Routing inputs in geometric configurations should be the corresponding invariants. Coarse/fine states use z_f=Pz_c and z_c=Qz_f; migrate all per-layer h/z, parameters, optimizer slots, readouts, and layouts together. QP=I preserves coarse representations only for specific constructions and does not promise lossless preservation of arbitrary detail. Representation trees do not arbitrarily create or delete real atoms/fingers.

EV01–EV08 handle prediction pairing, diagnostics, candidates, migration, competitive training, constraint checks, acceptance, and consolidation. Learning objectives include new data and old-task replay. Acceptance can be automatic at window boundaries, and large-scale merging can occur between generations. State updates, temporary refinement, and persistent learning are measured separately.

### 3.6 Task heads, JEPA, and scientific interfaces <a href="#section-026" id="section-026"></a>

E01/E02 output state and observation distributions; E03/E04 output contact and action candidates; E05 uses energy/force/stress or field heads; E06 uses biological count/function likelihoods; E07 generates conditional samples; and E08 performs suitable pooling or query readout. Energy derivatives require corresponding automatic differentiation and precision. Counts and physical quantities must not share an unqualified unitless loss.

JEPA forms a self-supervised path from teacher targets and a predictor, and its full anti-collapse recipe must be specified. JSBO connects latent representations to measurable states through observational supervision. Latent similarity does not automatically yield physical laws. ELN/LIMS, MD/DFT, and experimental devices are versioned external interfaces; tool outputs retain fidelity and error provenance.

### 3.7 Lyapunov mechanisms, reflection, and runtime <a href="#section-027" id="section-027"></a>

L01–L08 define the object of stability, V, drift, supply, correction, graph switching, training/counterexamples, and evidence respectively. V is independent of prediction heads; drift or feasibility is checked after a candidate is generated. A corrective action forms a new candidate that must be validated again. Computation cannot roll back a physical action that has already occurred.

G05/H08 review information available at the time, EV02 proposes causes, E01/H01 generates alternative rollouts, and EV05/G07 tests them against later data. Model counterfactuals are not real causal evidence; record cases where reflection changes a correct answer into an incorrect one and the cost of unhelpful retrospective analysis. Aegis-X's implementation proposal is revised to meet these functional requirements; an older product scope must not remove online learning.

## 4. Mathematical analysis framework <a href="#section-028" id="section-028"></a>

### 4.1 Functions/operators, constraint sets, and total error <a href="#section-029" id="section-029"></a>

Task inputs are a∈X, outputs are u∈Y, and K(a,c)={u:C=0,G≤0}. A solution set may be defined by F(a,u,c)=0 or argmin J; for multiple solutions, choose a conditional distribution or specify a solution branch. With sampling P_h and reconstruction R_h, the model approximates R_hMθP_h. A triangle inequality in the same output space can separate observation/discretization, representation, learning, and numerical errors. This does not mean all four terms are exactly identifiable online; see document 48.

Training uses component risks with explicit scales, L=Σ_rλ_rL_r. Calling this an evaluation functional does not change nonconvexity or guarantee physical meaning, identifiability, or good generalization.

### 4.2 Céa applies only to discrete subproblems satisfying its conditions <a href="#section-030" id="section-030"></a>

Let V be a Hilbert space; a(·,·), a bounded and coercive bilinear form with constants M,α>0; and ℓ, a continuous linear functional. Let V_h⊂V be a conforming finite-dimensional subspace and u_h its Galerkin solution. Then

$$
\|u-u_h\|_V\leq\frac M\alpha\inf_{v_h\in V_h}\|u-v_h\|_V.
$$

This quasi-optimal bound does not additionally require nested spaces for a single discrete problem, nor is it a general a posteriori estimator for neural networks. Only with nested levels is the best-approximation term non-increasing. Actual training error, nonlinear routing, approximate solves, and P/Q migration must be accounted for separately. The theorem cannot be transferred into a convergence proof for the entire SGD-Net system.

### 4.3 Objects and scope of Lyapunov conditions <a href="#section-031" id="section-031"></a>

For a defined error e, a reference positive-definite candidate is

$$
V(e)=e^T(R^TR+\epsilon I)e+\|g(e)-g(0)\|^2,\quad\epsilon>0,
$$
$$
V(e^+)-V(e)\leq-\alpha\|e\|^2+\sigma(\|w\|).
$$

Positive definiteness is not descent. Positive-definite upper/lower bounds, domain invariance, disturbance assumptions, and temporal conditions are required to establish the corresponding stability/boundedness. Arbitrary spectral clipping does not guarantee stability of the whole network or a switched system. Cross-graph checks of V_G'(Ps)≤μV_G(s)+η_switch additionally require a common V or appropriate switching/cumulative-budget rules; tolerances cannot accumulate indefinitely. Raw energy may increase under external work.

### 4.4 Implicit error and incomplete evidence <a href="#section-032" id="section-032"></a>

For a contraction mapping f with q<1 on a compatible domain, ||ζ−ζ*||≤||R(ζ)||/(1−q); in general, a small residual does not guarantee an accurate solution. For A=diag(1,ε), b=0, and candidate (0,1), the residual is ε while the error is 1, demonstrating why conditioning diagnostics cannot be omitted.

Unreachability in a knowledge graph indicates that a path is currently missing, not that a proposition is false; reachability likewise does not establish truth or causality. Safe-reasoning tasks should measure evidence completeness, abstention calibration, and false rejections instead of treating path constraints as a theorem eliminating hallucinations.

### 4.5 Expressiveness and resource boundaries <a href="#section-033" id="section-033"></a>

Restricted first-order message aggregation has graph-indistinguishability counterexamples, and finite-precision memory has information-capacity limits. U02/U03/U05 add global relationships, addressable memory, and higher-order representations as required by the task. U08 compatibility with original models is optional engineering. Evaluate representability, learnability, numerical accuracy, and acceptable cost separately.

- Metric/manifold-op trace, a long-term option solely for G-GeoS research;
- BICU GP/Cholesky/acquisition trace;
- Harness decision trace.

Optional metrics include operator shares, memory-access patterns, dynamic sparsity, fallback frequency, upper bounds on candidate hardware-IP benefits, and gaps relative to dense GPU/TPU baselines.

### 5.7 Scenario seven: Hybrid-solving and world-model bridge validation <a href="#section-056" id="section-056"></a>

#### Tasks <a href="#section-057" id="section-057"></a>

- `SolverRouter` selects different solvers across SSM/GNN/Tree/Harness/JSBO modules;
- `OnlineAdaptationController` uses EKF/UKF/filters to correct hidden states, thresholds, or small adapters;
- `JSBO` imports I-JEPA/V-JEPA latent representations and maps them to structured GraphState or PhysicalState;
- `CollectiveMergeManager` merges edge-side delta snapshots and validates regressions through Harness.

#### Comparison methods <a href="#section-058" id="section-058"></a>

- BP-only SGD-Net;
- SGD-Net without online filters;
- JEPA encoder baseline without JSBO;
- Direct FedAvg / Model Soup;
- Collective merging with TIES-like conflict resolution and distillation.

#### Metrics <a href="#section-059" id="section-059"></a>

- Solver-selection accuracy and fallback frequency;
- Whether online residuals decrease without degrading safety metrics;
- Bridge consistency error and physical residuals;
- Whether multitask metrics regress after merging;
- Reproducibility of `SolverTrace`, `AdaptationTrace`, and `ModelMergeTrace`.

This scenario validates only the engineering feasibility of hybrid solving, world-model interoperability, and collective feedback. It must not be interpreted as an experiment establishing AGI.

## 6. Algorithm pseudocode <a href="#section-060" id="section-060"></a>

### 6.1 Real events and temporary solving <a href="#section-061" id="section-061"></a>

```text
Freeze active model/graph/calibration/layout version ν
Validate input identity, units, clocks, masks, and provenance
Pair real outcomes with earlier predictions for the same target; innovation is unknown without a record
Encode and construct the graph; aggregate messages and update SSM physical time once per layer
Correct using valid innovations; output future-state/task candidates
Extract component diagnostics; choose no change, additional observation, or refinement within budget
If refining: copy candidate states, freeze versions, and execute explicit blocks or IM solves
Perform applicable Lyapunov/domain/resource checks; record failures and unknown status
Output a report or commit an action under its contract; store new pre-observation predictions
```

### 6.2 Retrospective analysis, local learning, and persistent acceptance <a href="#section-062" id="section-062"></a>

```text
Collect real outcomes, old-task replay, and information available at the time; label synthetic records separately
Generate competing explanations; check data quality, repeatability, and identifiability evidence
If insufficient representation is supported: propose bounded tree/graph/state/expert changes; otherwise choose the relevant correction
Migrate all parameters, per-layer states, optimizer, layout, and indices in the candidate copy
Train with new data and replay; keep versions fixed within each solve and backward pass
Compare parent/candidate quality, forgetting, and total cost on later independent data
Automatically accept after applicable equivariance, stability, migration, and resource conditions pass
Retain the active version on failure; continue monitoring and prune/distill/withdraw candidates according to evidence
```

This algorithm awaits implementation. A Python demonstration of random splitting alone cannot establish a complete continual-learning system.

## 7. Discussion <a href="#section-063" id="section-063"></a>

### 7.1 Potential benefits awaiting validation <a href="#section-064" id="section-064"></a>

- Concentrate computational resources in local high-residual regions;
- Use graph topology to reduce unconstrained fully connected drift;
- Organize history through bounded state recurrence in applicable tasks;
- Use dynamic trees for interpretable boundaries and local isolation;
- Introduce FEM/BEM-like error-control ideas into AI architectures.
- Reduce coordinate-frame dependence in three-dimensional scientific tasks through equivariant geometric propagation;
- Record and check candidates with SGD-Harness, supporting computational-version rollback and prescribed device handling; already executed physical actions cannot be rolled back;
- Use GeometryTrace/HarnessTrace as evidence for experimental reproduction, CModels, and hardware-opportunity assessment.

### 7.2 Limitations <a href="#section-065" id="section-065"></a>

- Rigorous theoretical proofs depend on relatively strong assumptions;
- Dynamic topology adds complexity to parallel computation and batching;
- Hard splits in differentiable trees may affect end-to-end training;
- In LLM tasks, factual-graph construction quality limits achievable performance;
- Patent and paper work require further reproducible experimental data.
- Equivariant GNNs and crystal-graph construction increase engineering complexity;
- Overly strict Harness policies may raise false interception rates, while overly permissive policies weaken safety benefits;
- BICU, Geometry Unit, and G-GeoS / Manifold-Metric Assist currently suit only trace/CModel-stage opportunity assessment; hardware gains must not be claimed without measurements;
- PIEVO-like active discovery involves experimental costs, data biases, and safety/compliance concerns, requiring phased validation.
- Hybrid solving adds scheduling complexity; `SolverRouter`, fallback, traces, and ablations are needed to establish benefits;
- JEPA/G-JEPA/AMI are world-model research directions only; the present paper cannot be written as proof that general intelligence has been implemented.

## 8. Conclusion <a href="#section-066" id="section-066"></a>

SGD-Net's central research direction is graph–temporal–local representation and computational adaptation driven by trustworthy error feedback. This paper specifies interfaces for high-dimensional equivariance, implicit solving, state migration, reflection, and continual learning, with conditional analysis and experimental plans. There are currently no measured results. It does not claim unified stable convergence, universally reduced hallucinations, superiority to all architectures, or achieved hardware gains. The mechanism should first be tested on one real task and its corresponding small mathematical task, then extended according to evidence.

## 9. Extended experiments for scientific foundation models <a href="#section-068" id="section-068"></a>

Based on the newly selected documents, the paper can extend its third experimental category to structure-preserving augmentation of scientific foundation models. This work should not claim to replace existing foundation models; it should augment their outputs, sampling processes, or local refinement.

### 9.1 AlphaFold 3 / all-atom complex augmentation <a href="#section-069" id="section-069"></a>

AlphaFold 3 can jointly predict complexes involving proteins, nucleic acids, small-molecule ligands, ions, and modified residues, using Pairformer and diffusion modules to generate atomic coordinates directly. Public materials also describe limitations involving static structures, insufficient coverage of multiple conformations, hallucinations in disordered regions, chirality errors, atomic clashes, and high antibody–antigen sampling costs.

SGD-Net can be investigated as an `SGD-Fold` augmentation layer through the following hypotheses:

- After converting AF3 output structures into all-atom heterogeneous graphs, GNNs can express chemical-bond, contact, interface, and ligand-pocket constraints more explicitly;
- Combining pLDDT, PAE, pTM, and ipTM with geometric/energy residuals can yield more interpretable posterior errors;
- Dynamic trees can add sampling, isolation, or refinement only in low-confidence or high-residual regions, reducing the cost of global multi-seed sampling.
- Equivariant geometric layers can reduce the effect of all-atom coordinate-frame choices on refinement;
- SGD-Harness can check clashes, chirality, bond geometry, and interface residuals before output structures enter docking, wet-lab experiments, or candidate reports.

Optional metrics include clash rate, chirality violations, pocket-aligned RMSD, DockQ, iLDDT, local pLDDT/PAE calibration, and local resampling cost.

### 9.2 RFdiffusion / protein-design augmentation <a href="#section-070" id="section-070"></a>

RFdiffusion is a guided diffusion model for protein design, supporting binder design, symmetric oligomers, enzyme active-site scaffolding, and motif scaffolding. As `SGD-Design`, SGD-Net can be used to study functional-motif boundary constraints, reverse-diffusion circuit breakers, and posterior candidate screening.

Experimental hypotheses include:

- GNN boundary constraints can reduce motif drift;
- Dynamic-tree interruption mechanisms can reject physically infeasible reverse-diffusion paths early;
- Posterior-error-driven local redesign can reduce whole-chain resampling.
- Harness can trigger rollback or local repair during generation instead of waiting until every complete candidate is generated before filtering.

Optional metrics include motif RMSD, interface score, Rosetta energy proxies, candidate enrichment, and expression/stability proxies.

### 9.3 ESMFold / protein-language-model augmentation <a href="#section-071" id="section-071"></a>

ESMFold represents the rapid structure-prediction approach based on protein language models. As `SGD-ESM`, SGD-Net can investigate SSM/Mamba replacement layers for long sequences, GNN contact-graph layers, and local OOD adaptation with dynamic trees.

Experimental hypotheses include:

- SSM/Mamba can reduce contextual complexity for very long protein sequences;
- GNN contact graphs can supply explicit spatial topology missing from purely sequential representations;
- Local dynamic-tree growth can alleviate OOD errors for new viral variants, artificial proteins, or orphan proteins.

### 9.4 Multimodal research agents and materials discovery <a href="#section-072" id="section-072"></a>

The fourth experimental category can target `SGD-Scientist`: construct a multimodal research graph from literature, material structures, DFT/VASP simulations, XRD/SEM characterization, and experimental results, then perform active learning with dynamic trees.

Optional metrics include candidate hit rate, simulation–experiment discrepancy, experimental round count, candidate diversity, pruning rate for failed experiments, and cost per successful candidate.

Materials and crystal tasks should prioritize `CrystalGraphBuilder` and the `crystal_egnn_refinement` benchmark. PIEVO/BO/IDS-like active-discovery tasks can record runtime for GP, Cholesky, acquisition, and Harness review to assess whether dedicated BICU hardware is worthwhile.

## 12. Alignment with the latest experimental and domain roadmaps <a href="#section-075" id="section-075"></a>
