The original discussion material (not included in this package) raises two new questions:

1. How does SGD-Net compare with Yann LeCun's JEPA / I-JEPA / V-JEPA world-model direction? Can SGD-Net and SGD-XPU/XSoC support it?
2. Can mathematical bridge operators be defined to “convert a general JEPA self-supervised protocol into a physical variational manifold,” and implemented at compiler and hardware levels?

This document answers:

> SGD-Net should view JEPA as a valuable self-supervised world-representation frontend, rather than a competitor. Through `JSBO` (JEPA-to-Structure-preserving Bridge Operator), SGD-Net can project, calibrate, and constrain JEPA latent predictions onto physical/topological/knowledge manifolds, forming a combined architecture of “general world representation + structure-preserving posterior control + dynamic self-evolution + heterogeneous hardware execution.”

Caution remains necessary:

- JEPA is an active research direction, not a final solution that has fully solved world modeling;
- SGD-Net likewise cannot claim “absolutely no collapse” or “complete realization of AGI”;
- JSBO should be defined as a verifiable family of bridge operators, not a universal formula;
- PPU/SGD-TPU/XSoC hardware should progress gradually through software simulation, profiling, kernels, and FPGA/board-level prototypes.

The original discussion material (not included in this package) adds two judgments: first, JEPA and SGD-Net integration can belong to an Advanced Machine Intelligence research roadmap, but must not be described as realized AGI; second, Transformer/LLM components should serve language, multimodal interfaces, and tool shells, rather than simply being rejected or replaced.

## 2. A Reliable Definition of JEPA <a href="#section-002" id="section-002"></a>

The core idea of JEPA (Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture) is:

> Predict representations of “missing parts” or “future states” in an abstract embedding space, rather than reconstructing raw data at the pixel, waveform, or token level.

A typical JEPA contains three core components:

| Component | Role | Typical implementation |
|---|---|---|
| Context Encoder | Encode visible context $$x$$ to obtain $$z_x$$ | ViT, 3D ViT, GNN, audio/trajectory encoder |
| Target Encoder | Encode target segment $$y$$ to obtain $$z_y$$ | Often an EMA target encoder or an encoder of the same architecture |
| Predictor | Predict $$\hat{z}_y$$ from $$z_x$$ and mask/position/action tokens | Small Transformer, MLP, linear/recurrent predictor |

The basic form can be written as:

$$
z_x=f_\theta(x),\qquad z_y=f_{\bar\theta}(y),\qquad \hat{z}_y=g_\phi(z_x,m,a)
$$

Training usually brings the predicted embedding close to the target embedding:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{JEPA}=d(\hat{z}_y,\operatorname{sg}(z_y))
$$

Here $$\operatorname{sg}$$ denotes stop-gradient, or no direct gradient backpropagation into the target network.

### 2.1 JEPA versus Generative Reconstruction <a href="#section-003" id="section-003"></a>

| Dimension | Generative reconstruction | JEPA |
|---|---|---|
| Objective | Reconstruct pixels/tokens/waveforms | Predict high-level embeddings |
| Advantages | Generation and direct visualization | Greater focus on semantics and predictable structure |
| Risks | Computation may be wasted reconstructing high-entropy details | Embedding collapse and weak latent-space interpretability |
| Suitable tasks | Image/text/video generation | Representation learning, world models, planning frontends |

I-JEPA predicts target-block representations from visible context blocks in images; V-JEPA extends this idea to video feature prediction; V-JEPA 2 further connects video self-supervised pretraining, video question answering, action-conditioned prediction, and robotic planning. Work such as C-JEPA notes that JEPA still needs to address representation collapse, inadequate mean prediction, and regularization stability.

### 2.2 Boundaries of Gaussian/Whitening Distribution Constraints in JEPA <a href="#section-004" id="section-004"></a>

Three points must be distinguished:

1. **Native I-JEPA / V-JEPA does not primarily target an explicit VAE-style Gaussian prior**. The public I-JEPA/V-JEPA line commonly uses target networks, stop-gradient, mask prediction, and embedding distance losses to predict high-level latents, rather than requiring every latent to follow $$\mathcal{N}(0,I)$$ strictly.
2. **Related JEPA anticollapse work uses variance, covariance, rank, whitening, or entropy regularization**. VICReg / Barlow Twins / SIGReg / C-JEPA-style ideas often prevent collapse by ensuring sufficient per-dimension variance, low interdimension correlation, or nearly diagonal covariance. This resembles “normalization/whitening” in engineering, but is not equivalent to “having modeled a complete Gaussian posterior.”
3. **JSBO should treat Gaussian modeling as an optional probabilistic calibration layer**. When uncertainty propagation, Kalman/EKF/UKF, Bayesian optimization, or physical residual confidence is needed, JEPA latents may be approximated as locally Gaussian:

$$
z \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_z,\Sigma_z)
$$

and propagated after the bridge mapping as:

$$
s=\Psi_\omega(z),\qquad
\Sigma_s \approx J_\Psi(z)\Sigma_zJ_\Psi(z)^T
$$

This adds an auditable uncertainty interface to SGD-Net/JSBO; it does not mean JEPA itself has solved probabilistic world modeling.

### 2.3 V-JEPA 2 and Physical Reasoning Benchmark Boundaries <a href="#section-005" id="section-005"></a>

V-JEPA 2's positive contribution is that self-supervised video world models are beginning to connect understanding, prediction, and short-horizon robotic planning, demonstrating potential for action-conditioned training with relatively few robot videos. However, recent physical/causal/counterfactual benchmarks such as IntPhys 2, Minimal Video Pairs, and CausalVQA also show that current multimodal world models still lag humans substantially in intuitive physics, long-horizon planning, counterfactual prediction, and understanding actionability.

This document therefore uses the following evidence levels:

| Proposition | Current evidence | Wording in this document |
|---|---|---|
| JEPA helps learn high-level world representations | Supported by I-JEPA, V-JEPA, V-JEPA 2, and related papers | Can serve as a world-model frontend |
| JEPA can support short-horizon robotic planning | V-JEPA 2 demonstrates preliminary capabilities | A research and PoC direction |
| JEPA has solved physical world modeling | Physical benchmarks still show substantial gaps | Must not be stated |
| SGD-Net + JSBO can address insufficient JEPA structural/physical constraints | Currently a system architecture hypothesis | Requires JEPA-Bridge Replay and physical residual validation |

### 2.4 Boundaries of LeJEPA World-Model Identifiability Theory <a href="#section-006" id="section-006"></a>

The 2026 paper *When Does LeJEPA Learn a World Model?* (David Klindt, Yann LeCun, Randall Balestriero) adds a particularly important but explicitly conditional mathematical result to the JEPA direction. Its conclusion cannot be reduced to “JEPA naturally learns the real world.” More accurately:

> In a class of worlds with independent Gaussian latent variables, stationary additive-noise transitions, and nonlinear observation mixing, if LeJEPA satisfies both its alignment objective and Gaussian distribution constraints, the learned representation corresponds linearly/orthogonally to the true latent variables, differing only by a global rotation or reflection.

The paper can be summarized as follows:

| Theoretical point | Meaning | Constraint on this document |
|---|---|---|
| Alignment + Gaussian regularization | LeJEPA minimizes positive-pair embedding distances while using methods such as SIGReg to push the embedding distribution toward an isotropic Gaussian | `var/cov` alone is insufficient; add SIGReg/Gaussianity validation |
| Linear identifiability | For Gaussian latent worlds, optimal representations satisfy $$h(z)=Qz$$ with $$Q\in O(n)$$ | JSBO must validate “linear recoverability,” not just downstream error |
| Up to rotation/reflection | Orthogonal-transform ambiguity is allowed between learned world latents and true latent variables | Evaluate with Orthogonal Procrustes / rotation-aligned $$R^2$$ |
| Gaussian uniqueness | Within stationary additive-noise worlds, Gaussian is the unique latent distribution guaranteeing linear identifiability | Arbitrary empirical distributions cannot be forced into a standard-normal interpretation |
| Sturm-Liouville converse | For general distributions, the transition operator's first nontrivial eigenfunction typically gives only monotonic identifiability; linear identifiability requires an affine eigenfunction | Spectral analysis/score-linearity tests are needed as research validation tools |
| Score linearity condition | An affine eigenfunction forces the score function $$(\log p)'$$ to be linear, yielding a Gaussian density | Use as a “Gaussian uniqueness” diagnostic, not a default engineering assumption |
| Approximate identifiability | Identifiability error degrades smoothly when alignment and whitening are only approximately satisfied | Monitor train/validation gaps and bounds to avoid overfitting |
| Optimal latent planning | If $$h(z)=Qz$$ and control costs are $$O(n)$$-invariant, learned latent planning is equivalent to true latent planning | Planning guarantees depend on cost symmetry and theorem assumptions; do not generalize to all tasks |
| DMC Reacher experiments | Pixel Reacher under Gaussian OU sampling is closer to identifiable; real policy trajectories violate assumptions through non-Gaussianity, anisotropy, and other effects | Robot trajectories require distribution audits and ablations |

Thus, **the current JSBO/SGD-Net architecture has the interface foundation to accommodate LeJEPA theory, but cannot yet claim “perfect support” for its proof conditions**. It needs identifiability verification, SIGReg/Gaussian regularization, distribution audits, Sturm-Liouville/score diagnostics, and DMC Reacher/control benchmark adapters to advance from “architectural compatibility” to “experimentally and theoretically verifiable conditions.”

## 3. Relationship between JEPA and SGD-Net <a href="#section-008" id="section-008"></a>

JEPA and SGD-Net complement one another at different layers, rather than simply replacing one another.

| Dimension | JEPA | SGD-Net |
|---|---|---|
| Core paradigm | Self-supervised latent prediction | State space + graph topology + dynamic trees + posterior control |
| Main strength | Learn semantic/dynamic representations without reconstructing high-entropy details | Explicit integration of physics, topology, posterior error, sources, and stability constraints |
| Main risk | Representation collapse, weak latent metrics, still-developing long-horizon planning | Architectural complexity, implementation difficulty, need for explicit scenario constraints |
| Best role | World-representation frontend and visual/video/multimodal encoder | Structure-preserving reasoning core, control loop, dynamic self-evolution system |
| Integration | Output $$z_t,\hat{z}_{t+k}$$ | Map them into GraphState / PhysicalState / FastPathCandidate |

In one sentence:

> JEPA provides a way to “learn world representations”; SGD-Net provides a system architecture for “operating these representations safely in physical, scientific, industrial, and self-evolution loops.”

Transformers, ViTs, 3D ViTs, and LLMs retain important roles in this combination: language interfaces, tool invocation, multimodal tokenization, encoder/decoder shells, and human-machine collaboration. This document challenges the sufficiency assumption that “autoregressive token prediction alone covers physical world modeling and closed-loop control,” rather than rejecting the Transformer ecosystem.

## 5. Core Addition: The JSBO Bridge Operator <a href="#section-011" id="section-011"></a>

### 5.1 Name and Boundaries <a href="#section-012" id="section-012"></a>

Extend the original discussion's “JEPA-to-Symplectic Bridge Operator” to the more careful definition:

```text
JSBO = JEPA-to-Structure-preserving Bridge Operator
```

In Hamiltonian/physically symplectifiable systems, JSBO can specialize to:

```text
JEPA-to-Symplectic Bridge Operator
```

This avoids forcing every problem into a symplectic geometric system.

### 5.2 Space Definitions <a href="#section-013" id="section-013"></a>

Let JEPA latent space be:

$$
\mathcal{H}_{J}=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d_J}\}
$$

where:

$$
z_t=f_\theta(x_t),\qquad \hat{z}_{t+1}=g_\phi(z_t,a_t)
$$

Let the SGD-Net target structure space be:

$$
\mathcal{M}_{SGD}=\mathcal{M}_{phys}\cup\mathcal{M}_{graph}\cup\mathcal{M}_{logic}
$$

Here:

- $$\mathcal{M}_{phys}$$: physical phase spaces, PDE state spaces, conservation manifolds;
- $$\mathcal{M}_{graph}$$: entity/sensor/mesh/material/molecular graphs;
- $$\mathcal{M}_{logic}$$: knowledge graphs, constraints, sources, and verification states.

JSBO is a family of learnable, constrainable mappings:

$$
\Psi_\omega:\mathcal{H}_{J}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{SGD}
$$

The output need not be a single vector; it may be a structured state:

```text
PhysicalLatentState:
  q, p or field variables
  graph_nodes, graph_edges
  metric, source_tag, uncertainty
  invariant_report
```

## 6. Core Mathematical Objectives of JSBO <a href="#section-014" id="section-014"></a>

JSBO is a set of losses and constraints, rather than one formula.

### 6.1 JEPA Prediction Consistency <a href="#section-015" id="section-015"></a>

Preserve the original JEPA prediction objective:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{pred}=d\left(g_\phi(f_\theta(x)), f_{\bar\theta}(y)\right)
$$

This regularization objective reduces bridge distortion. Representation collapse, task error, and bridge identifiability must be checked; it does not guarantee preservation of all JEPA capabilities.

### 6.2 Structural Mapping Consistency <a href="#section-016" id="section-016"></a>

Map JEPA predictions and targets separately:

$$
\hat{s}_{t+1}=\Psi_\omega(\hat{z}_{t+1}),\qquad s_{t+1}=\Psi_\omega(z_{t+1})
$$

Require consistency in structure space as well:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{bridge}=D_{SGD}(\hat{s}_{t+1},s_{t+1})
$$

Here $$D_{SGD}$$ may be:

- Graph distance;
- Physical field norms;
- Constraint violation penalties;
- Source/confidence consistency;
- Downstream task error.

### 6.3 Metric Pullback Constraints <a href="#section-017" id="section-017"></a>

JEPA latent space does not necessarily have a physical metric by default. JSBO should learn or estimate a target manifold metric $$M(s)$$ and pull it back into latent space through the Jacobian:

$$
G_J(z)=J_\Psi(z)^T M(\Psi(z))J_\Psi(z)
$$

where:

$$
J_\Psi(z)=\frac{\partial\Psi_\omega(z)}{\partial z}
$$

This aligns latent distances with physical/topological meaning, rather than relying only on Euclidean distance.

### 6.4 Hamiltonian/Symplectic Structure Constraints <a href="#section-018" id="section-018"></a>

If the target system has phase space $$s=(q,p)$$, its standard symplectic matrix is:

$$
\Omega=\begin{pmatrix}0&I\\-I&0\end{pmatrix}
$$

A symplectic residual can be added:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{symp}=\left\|J_\Psi(z)^T\Omega J_\Psi(z)-\Omega\right\|_F^2
$$

This term applies only to conservative mechanics, Hamiltonian systems, or approximately symplectic scenarios. Dissipative systems should instead use energy-dissipation, Lyapunov, or PDE residual constraints.

### 6.5 Koopman Consistency <a href="#section-019" id="section-019"></a>

A JEPA predictor can be viewed as latent-space dynamics:

$$
\hat{z}_{t+1}=P_\phi(z_t,a_t)
$$

JSBO can introduce a Koopman linearization approximation:

$$
K\Psi_\omega(z_t)\approx \Psi_\omega(P_\phi(z_t,a_t))
$$

Corresponding loss:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{koop}=\left\|K\Psi_\omega(z_t)-\Psi_\omega(P_\phi(z_t,a_t))\right\|_2^2
$$

Choose spectral constraints by scenario:

| Scenario | Suggested spectral constraint |
|---|---|
| Conservative system | $$|\lambda_i(K)|\approx1$$ |
| Dissipative system | $$|\lambda_i(K)|\le1$$ |
| Controlled-growth system | Local $$>1$$ is allowed, but a Lyapunov or energy upper bound is required |

It is therefore inappropriate to universally require “all eigenvalues strictly on the unit circle.”

### 6.6 Physical Energy and PDE Residual Alignment <a href="#section-020" id="section-020"></a>

If a PPU or highly trustworthy simulator can compute physical energy/residuals, add:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{energy}=\left\|\mathcal{H}(\Psi_\omega(\hat{z}_{t+1}))-E_{obs}\right\|_2^2
$$

and:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{pde}=\left\|\mathcal{N}(u_\Psi)-b\right\|_{\Omega}^2+\lambda_{bc}\left\|\mathcal{B}(u_\Psi)-g\right\|_{\partial\Omega}^2
$$

Here $$\mathcal{N}$$ is the PDE operator and $$\mathcal{B}$$ the boundary operator.

### 6.7 Collapse and Redundancy Control <a href="#section-021" id="section-021"></a>

Following C-JEPA/VICReg/SIGReg-style ideas, JSBO should preserve representation diversity:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{var}=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\max(0,\gamma-\sigma_k(z))
$$

$$
\mathcal{L}_{cov}=\sum_{i\ne j}Cov(z)_{ij}^2
$$

### 6.8 Gaussian/Whitening Distribution Constraints and Uncertainty Propagation <a href="#section-022" id="section-022"></a>

Document 18 previously included only `var/cov` anticollapse terms, potentially suggesting that JSBO ignores distribution constraints associated with JEPA. More accurately:

- **Basic layer**: retain variance, covariance, rank, and whitening monitoring to prevent all latents collapsing to a constant vector;
- **Probabilistic layer**: approximate batch or local latents as Gaussian when needed, supplying $$\mu_z$$, $$\Sigma_z$$, or low-rank covariance;
- **Structural layer**: propagate uncertainty through JSBO into `GraphState / PhysicalState`;
- **Control layer**: SGD-Harness, PPU residuals, Kalman/EKF/UKF, or BICU/BO modules consume this uncertainty.

With a standard-normal whitening prior, add:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{gauss}=D\left(q_\theta(z\mid x)\,\|\,\mathcal{N}(0,I)\right)
$$

Here $$D$$ can be KL, MMD, sliced Wasserstein, or moment-matching distance. For second-order whitening alone:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{white}=\|\mu_z\|_2^2+\|\Sigma_z-I\|_F^2
$$

Physical systems should not mechanically require every latent to be standard normal. Local or conditional Gaussians are more prudent:

$$
z_t\mid c_t \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_z(c_t),\Sigma_z(c_t))
$$

Write post-bridge uncertainty into `BridgeState`:

```text
BridgeState:
  latent_distribution:
    family: gaussian | diagonal_gaussian | low_rank_gaussian | empirical
    mean_ref: TensorRef
    covariance_ref: TensorRef | LowRankCovRef | None
    calibration_score: float
```

This layer upgrades JEPA “point-estimate latents” to “structural states with confidence intervals” for posterior error, risk control, and active exploration. It remains an **optional probabilistic calibration layer**, not a mandatory definition of native JEPA.

The final training objective can be written as:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{JSBO}=\mathcal{L}_{pred}
+\alpha\mathcal{L}_{bridge}
+\beta\mathcal{L}_{metric}
+\gamma\mathcal{L}_{symp/lyap}
+\delta\mathcal{L}_{koop}
+\mu\mathcal{L}_{energy/pde}
+\nu\mathcal{L}_{var/cov}
+\rho\mathcal{L}_{gauss/white}
$$

### 6.9 LeJEPA Identifiability Constraints and JSBO Extensions <a href="#section-023" id="section-023"></a>

To support the LeJEPA paper's “world-model identifiability” conclusions, JSBO must separate representation usefulness into three questions:

1. **Does the distribution satisfy theoretical premises?** Are latents approximately isotropic Gaussian? Do positive pairs come from stationary additive-noise / OU-like transitions?
2. **Is the representation linearly/orthogonally identifiable?** Can learned latents align with true/simulated latents through one orthogonal matrix $$Q$$?
3. **Is planning faithful?** Do trajectories, actions, and costs planned in learned latent space agree with true latent space or a highly trustworthy simulator?

LeJEPA's core constraint can be written as:

$$
\min_h\;\mathbb{E}\left[\|h(z')-h(z)\|_2^2\right]
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad h(z)\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)
$$

In the ideal Gaussian world, the optimum satisfies:

$$
h(z)=Qz,\qquad Q\in O(n)
$$

Engineering evaluation should not directly compare $$h(z)$$ with $$z$$; first solve orthogonal Procrustes alignment:

$$
Q^*=\arg\min_{Q^TQ=I}\|HQ-Z\|_F^2
$$

and record:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{id}=1-R^2(HQ^*,Z)+\lambda\|{Q^*}^TQ^*-I\|_F^2
$$

For a converse diagnostic of Gaussian uniqueness, estimate the latent score:

$$
s_p(z)=\nabla_z\log p(z)
$$

and check whether an affine/linear form explains it:

$$
s_p(z)\approx Az+b
$$

A large residual suggests that “Gaussian uniqueness/linear identifiability” premises likely fail. Fall back to empirical, mixture, or local conditional Gaussian modeling instead of forcing the LeJEPA theorem to apply.

| LeJEPA theoretical point | Current SGD-Net status | Required extension | Validation |
|---|---|---|---|
| SIGReg / isotropic Gaussian regularization | `var/cov/white/gauss` concepts exist, but SIGReg is not distinguished from second-order whitening | `SIGRegGaussianRegularizer`, supporting sketched Gaussianity, MMD/CF distances, and second-order whitening fallback | Gaussianity score, batch/stream drift, regularizer-weight sweep |
| Linear identifiability | JSBO has bridge consistency but does not verify $$h(z)=Qz$$ | `LeJEPAIdentifiabilityVerifier`, `OrthogonalProcrustesAligner` | Rotation-aligned $$R^2$$, orthogonality residual, linear probe gap |
| “Only a rotation/reflection apart” | No dedicated evaluation item previously | Record `OrthogonalLatentReport` in traces | $$\|Q^TQ-I\|_F$$, det/sign, per-dimension $$R^2$$ |
| Gaussian uniqueness and non-Gaussian counterexamples | “Gaussian assumption misuse” risk exists, but ablations are missing | `DistributionAblationSuite`: generalized normal, Laplace, uniform, mixture, empirical trajectories | Whether recovery peaks at Gaussian; whether non-Gaussian fallback triggers |
| Sturm-Liouville spectral explanation | No spectral diagnostic tools currently | `SturmLiouvilleSpectralAnalyzer` to verify whether the first eigenfunction is affine in toy worlds/low-dimensional simulations | Eigenfunction monotonicity/affineness residual |
| Score / log-density derivative linearity | No score test currently | `ScoreLinearityTest`, estimating $$\nabla\log p(z)$$ and fitting an affine score | Score linearity residual, normality diagnostic |
| Approximate identifiability / overfitting | Replay and residuals exist, but no bound monitor | `ApproxIdentifiabilityBoundMonitor`, train/validation identifiability gap | Bound violations, train-validation $$R^2$$ gap, overfitting alerts |
| DMC Reacher / pixel control | No standard adapter yet | `ReacherPixelBenchmarkAdapter`, latent planning evaluator | OU versus trajectory controls, planning cost, nearest-neighbor decoding error |
| Optimal latent planning | FeedforwardPredictor/ControlArbiter exist, but $$O(n)$$-invariant cost conditions are unverified | `LatentPlanningEquivarianceCheck`, cost symmetry audit | Learned-latent versus oracle cost difference, action-sequence agreement |

Extend the total objective to:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{JSBO+LeJEPA}=\mathcal{L}_{JSBO}
+\tau\mathcal{L}_{SIGReg}
+\kappa\mathcal{L}_{id}
+\xi\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{score\text{-}linear}}
+\omega\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{approx\text{-}bound}}
$$

The last two terms mainly support research validation and gates and need not be enabled in all production training.

## 9. Dream Consolidation and Creative Scenarios <a href="#section-028" id="section-028"></a>

The original discussion material (not included in this package) also asks: although SGD-Net primarily pursues industrial and scientific trustworthiness, can portions of its offline replay, counterfactual perturbation, and dynamic organization mechanisms be extracted for writing novels?

Yes, as a **creative module in a nonindustrial mode**, but the constraint functions must change.

### 9.1 Industrial versus Creative Modes <a href="#section-029" id="section-029"></a>

| Dimension | Industrial/scientific mode | Creative/narrative mode |
|---|---|---|
| Main constraints | Physical conservation, source trustworthiness, posterior error | World-building consistency, character motivation, emotional tension, resolving foreshadowing |
| PPU role | FP64/PDE/energy ground-truth barrier | Replaceable with a Narrative Critic |
| GNN role | Physical/knowledge/experimental graphs | Character relationships, event causality, thematic imagery graphs |
| Dynamic trees | Local refinement / risk isolation | Plot branching, conflict escalation, subplot pruning |
| Retrospection | Failure review and fast-path consolidation | Draft review, scene reorganization, cliché pruning |

Writing fiction should therefore use the following instead of full industrial PPU constraints:

```text
NarrativeGraph + CharacterState + PlotTree + ThemeCritic + Retrospection
```

### 9.2 Narrative Dream Operators <a href="#section-030" id="section-030"></a>

Define a “dream perturbation”:

$$
\Xi_{dream}\sim\mathcal{D}_{motif,emotion,conflict}
$$

Perturb the narrative graph:

$$
\mathcal{G}_{story}'=\operatorname{Perturb}(\mathcal{G}_{story},\Xi_{dream})
$$

Then require narrative consistency:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{story}=\lambda_1\mathcal{L}_{character}
+\lambda_2\mathcal{L}_{causality}
+\lambda_3\mathcal{L}_{theme}
+\lambda_4\mathcal{L}_{novelty}
-\lambda_5\mathcal{L}_{cliche}
$$

Here:

- $$\mathcal{L}_{character}$$: whether behavior follows character trajectories;
- $$\mathcal{L}_{causality}$$: whether plot causality closes;
- $$\mathcal{L}_{theme}$$: whether themes and imagery are consistent;
- $$\mathcal{L}_{novelty}$$: whether meaningful new combinations arise;
- $$\mathcal{L}_{cliche}$$: whether the story falls into clichéd scenes.

### 9.3 Boundary Statement <a href="#section-031" id="section-031"></a>

This “novel-writing” capability is not the main objective of the industrial SGD-Net system, nor does it imply innate human emotions. It simply means:

> Retrospection's offline replay, counterfactual perturbation, path pruning, and causal skeleton extraction can transfer to nonphysical scenarios; replacing physical constraints with narrative constraints can form a creative subsystem.

## 11. G-JEPA as a Research Hypothesis <a href="#section-033" id="section-033"></a>

In [SGD-Net Hybrid Solving and Training System and World-Model Research Roadmap](27.md), `G-JEPA` can be defined as:

> A joint-embedding predictive framework explicitly incorporating equivariance, manifold metrics, reachability, physical residuals, and structural state constraints into JEPA's latent prediction objective.

Its objective can be written as:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{GJEPA}=d_{\mathcal{M}}(\hat{z}_{t+k},z_{t+k})
+\alpha\mathcal{L}_{equiv}
+\beta\mathcal{L}_{metric}
+\gamma\mathcal{L}_{physics}
+\delta\mathcal{L}_{causal}
$$

Here $$d_{\mathcal{M}}$$ may be Euclidean, hyperbolic, Lorentz, a product manifold, or a learned metric. G-JEPA implementation must begin with toy worlds, small crystal/molecule tasks, short-horizon robotic planning, or video physics benchmarks, recording `MetricTrace`, `ManifoldTrace`, and `SolverTrace`. Until these validations are complete, it is only a research hypothesis and candidate divisional patent topic, not a universal AGI loss.

## 15. Risks and Practical Boundaries <a href="#section-037" id="section-037"></a>

| Risk | Explanation | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Overclaiming | Cannot say SGD-Net has “fully implemented JEPA world models” | Say “supports/integrates/constrains/augments JEPA representations” |
| Physicalization failure | Some JEPA latents may not be identifiable with physical variables | Calibrate JSBO with paired sensor, simulation, or experimental data |
| Symplectic structure misuse | Not all systems are Hamiltonian | Use Lyapunov, PDE residual, or statistical constraints for dissipative/stochastic systems |
| Collapse remains possible | Structural constraints cannot replace representation diversity regularization | Retain VICReg/SIGReg/rank/variance monitoring |
| Gaussian assumption misuse | Treating JEPA latents as strictly standard normal by default | Use an “optional Gaussian/whitening calibration layer” with empirical/mixture/low-rank fallback |
| LeJEPA theorem assumption misuse | Extending a stationary additive-noise + Gaussian latent proof to arbitrary physical/industrial systems | Create a theory assumption report checking Gaussianity, stationarity, additive noise, and latent dimension |
| Misleading “perfect support” | SGD-Net currently has architectural interfaces, without complete SIGReg, identifiability validation, or Reacher benchmarks | Say “extensible support/support awaiting validation” |
| Non-Gaussian real trajectories | Real policy trajectories such as DMC Reacher may be non-Gaussian, anisotropic, or have boundary wrapping | OU versus trajectory controls, distribution audits, and fallback |
| Overfitting masks identifiability | Good training-set latent alignment does not imply stable recovery of world latent variables | Add train-validation identifiability gaps, approximate bound monitoring, and OOD trajectory tests |
| Premature hardware promises | Workloads may not stabilize before JSBO hardware implementation | Software simulation, profiling, and kernels first; FPGA/board-level validation afterward |
| Creative-scenario confusion | Fiction writing is not industrial SGD-Net's core commercial objective | Optional narrative subsystem, excluded from the safety-control backbone |
| Excessive AGI narrative | V-JEPA 2 advances do not mean world modeling is solved | Use “AMI/AGI research path,” “candidate architecture,” and “benchmarks awaiting validation” |
| Unbalanced Transformer positioning | Simply rejecting Transformers ignores the practical engineering ecosystem | State their value for language, multimodality, tool invocation, and system shells |

## 16. Conclusion <a href="#section-038" id="section-038"></a>

The original discussion material's core contribution (not included in this package) is to connect SGD-Net to world-model research and propose an important extension:

```text
JEPA learns latent world representations
→ JSBO maps latents onto structure-preserving manifolds
→ SGD-Net reasons and controls through graphs, states, and dynamic trees
→ PPU/posterior error supplies physical and safety constraints
→ Retrospection dynamically organizes valuable bridge paths into fast paths
→ XPU/XSoC supplies low-latency heterogeneous execution
```

The best relationship between SGD-Net and JEPA is therefore:

> JEPA learns predictable abstract world representations from massive unlabeled data; SGD-Net places those representations into verifiable, constrainable, reflective, hardware-executable scientific and industrial loops.

For the latest LeJEPA identifiability theory, this document adopts a stricter engineering conclusion: SGD-Net/JSBO **can be extended to support** a validation loop encompassing alignment + SIGReg + Gaussian uniqueness + orthogonal identifiability + latent planning, but cannot currently claim “perfect support.” Only after extensions including `SIGRegGaussianRegularizer`, `LeJEPAIdentifiabilityVerifier`, `Score/Sturm-Liouville Analyzer`, `DistributionAblationSuite`, and `ReacherPixelBenchmarkAdapter` are complete and pass trace-driven benchmark gates can the capability advance from “architectural compatibility” to “validated support.”

This adds a new strategic layer to SGD-Net: a **world-model interoperability layer**. It can connect external representation models such as I-JEPA/V-JEPA and provide a unified structure-preserving bridge for future multimodal world models, robot video models, and self-supervised industrial sensor models.

For AGI/AMI narratives, the final conclusion remains cautious: JEPA + JSBO + SGD-Net + G-GeoS is an advanced machine intelligence direction worth researching, but its effectiveness must be validated progressively through physical reasoning, robotic planning, scientific experimental loops, and trace-driven CModel evaluation.
