## 1. Assessment <a href="#section-001" id="section-001"></a>

Integration is possible, and the three sets of materials have complementary foundations: SGD-Net defines a learnable spatiotemporal structural kernel; GLS research distinguishes model prediction, error evidence, and action execution; Aegis-X and HLO-OP-IR provide bounded computation, data lifecycles, and deployment validation methods. Narrow this to **SGD-Net-TC: a Topology- and Contact-Conditioned World Model**, a research variant of SGD-Net, initially studying cross-hand manipulation and then testing transfer to other constrained-dynamics tasks.

However, recombining “SSM + GNN + dynamic trees + JEPA + safety gates” does not establish a new algorithm. Earlier SGD-Net drafts already proposed most components, while external research includes graph-memory world models, graph dynamics, embodiment-conditioned prediction, and adaptive computation. New contributions must take the form of learnable state transformations, losses, and decision rules, validated against directly competing baselines. Hardware mappings and audit contracts have engineering value but cannot replace model contributions.

The central question recommended for validation is: **When embodiments differ, interaction topology changes, and compute is limited, can a model migrate existing memory, preserve action semantics for the same physical task, and correctly choose between more computation, more observations, and stopping the current candidate?**

## 3. Combinations That Are Not New Research Gaps <a href="#section-003" id="section-003"></a>

| Related work | Mechanisms already covered | Constraints on novelty claims |
|---|---|---|
| Mamba, MaIL | Selective state spaces, robot imitation policies | Replacing Transformers with SSMs is insufficient by itself for originality [^R01][^R02] |
| FACTS | World models with graph-structured memory, routing, and selective state propagation | “Graph + SSM + world model” has a direct precedent [^R03] |
| Graph Network Simulator, EGNN | Graph interaction dynamics and geometric equivariance | Graph physics and equivariance were not first proposed by this project [^R04][^R05] |
| Scaling Cross-Embodiment World Models for Dexterous Manipulation | Cross-embodiment graph world models and planning interfaces using particle states/displacements | “World models unifying different hands” already has a directly competing approach; arXiv v3 representation and planning methods have been checked [^R06] |
| GraphOp-WM | Morphology-related structural operators and reusable dynamic bases | Morphology/shared-dynamics decomposition has precedents; the paper covers parameter changes within related robot families [^R07] |
| GraspGraphNet | Explicit URDF graphs, dynamic hand–object edges, native grasp configurations | Embodiment graphs and dynamic edges alone are not new contributions [^R08] |
| CGP, T-Rex, RLDX-1 | Contact prediction, tactile time series, multiple streams, and memory | Contact prediction and fast/slow policies require mechanism-level comparisons [^R09][^R10][^R11] |
| DPP | Human/robot keypoint representations and video transfer | Intermediate keypoints do not establish dynamic executability on new hand types [^R12] |
| V-JEPA 2, LeJEPA theory | Predictive representations, short-horizon planning, conditional identifiability | JEPA bridges require renewed validation of physical variables, errors, and identification conditions [^R13][^R14] |
| Adaptive Computation Time | Input-dependent computational allocation | More computation for larger errors is insufficient as an entire new mechanism [^R15] |

Only **candidate combined contributions** can currently be proposed; literature priority has not been established, and no patent-novelty search has been performed. R06's arXiv v3 representation and methods text has now been additionally checked; before first experiments, its complete experimental protocol and available artifacts still require review to avoid bypassing the closest competitor. Related work “not describing a mechanism” does not mean it “definitely lacks that mechanism.”

## 4. Three Integration Options <a href="#section-004" id="section-004"></a>

| Option | Content | Benefits | Costs and limitations | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A: External supervisor | Retain an external VLA and add only SGD/GLS checks and records | Fastest system baseline; replaceable models | Mainly a runtime contribution, without a new predictive kernel | Required control and implementation starting point |
| B: Joint training of structural world model and action branch | SGD spatiotemporal kernel + embodiment graph + contact prediction + bounded refinement; external language/vision may be frozen | Isolates model-level benefits and limits training scale | Requires real action/contact data and ablations for coupled mechanisms | **Recommended main research track: SGD-Net-TC** |
| C: Train an all-modality general model from scratch | Build VLM, video world model, hand policy, online self-evolution, and chip | Theoretical full-stack control | Enormous data/training/validation budgets, difficult attribution, currently unsupported | Not selected now |

Option B can evolve from A in stages; research success does not require replacing strong pretrained perception or language models. New models should output testable future states and actions, not merely “evidence fields.”

## 5. Narrowing Candidate Innovations to Three Falsifiable Propositions <a href="#section-005" id="section-005"></a>

**H1: Consistency constraints on memory and task outputs before/after graph refinement reduce prediction jumps at switching.** Original document 13 already initializes new-node states, but restriction/prolongation operators, stable entity IDs, and cross-resolution task consistency still need definitions. The new proposal learns state migration and checks both instantaneous semantic consistency and subsequent predictive benefit, without assuming every refinement improves results.

**H2: Separating reducible computational error, missing observations, and model uncertainty allocates inference budgets more effectively than a single residual threshold.** More iterations cannot conjure ground truth from an occluded sensor. The proposed budgeter chooses refinement, additional candidates, observation requests, or candidate termination, while retaining irreducible action-checking and degraded-operation budgets. Benefits must be validated under equal total compute budgets.

**H3: Joint constraints on embodiment-conditioned action decoding and short-horizon contact prediction support new-hand adaptation better than pose alignment alone.** Transfer targets are task-relevant object changes and contact effects; the target hand still requires its own kinematics, transmission, sensor calibration, and controller. Joint losses may reduce unnecessary adaptation data or increase training conflicts; zero-shot performance cannot be promised beforehand.

H1 is not simply adding a graph module; H2 does not remove fixed-budget safety checks; H3 does not force different robotic hands into identical joint angles. The combination worth testing is these three propositions co-designed with state transactions and compilation budgets.

## 6. Conceptual Conflicts to Correct First <a href="#section-006" id="section-006"></a>

1. **Do not mix three topologies.** Predictors cannot arbitrarily add/remove robotic-hand link topology; current contact edges change with physical state; inference-graph refinement only changes representational resolution. Knowledge/task-graph edges are even less equivalent to causal edges.
2. **Do not mix three times.** Number sensor physical time, internal model rollout time, and solver iterations within one forward pass separately. Additional SSM computation must not record “imagination” as real history.
3. **State change is not error.** Adjacent hidden-state differences in older drafts may reflect real motion; online residuals must align earlier predictions with current observations. Without observations, only uncertainty can be estimated.
4. **Per-step spectral radius below 1 does not guarantee switched-system stability.** For example, `[[0,2],[0,0]]` and `[[0,0],[2,0]]` each have spectral radius 0, but their alternating product has eigenvalue 4. Stronger conditions such as common-norm contraction may bound internal recurrence, but still do not prove mechanical-system safety.
5. **Do not project every node to smaller values.** Joint measurements, mass, energy, and object positions retain physical units; implement hidden-representation regularization separately from physical-feasible-set projection.
6. **Graphs/SSMs do not inherently satisfy every symmetry.** Temporal scans run along time and must not depend on arbitrary node-number ordering; robot gravity, joint axes, and chirality restrict available rotational/reflection symmetries.
7. **Stability or symplecticity cannot unify every contact.** Friction, collisions, and dissipation generally require piecewise modes and external-force inputs, rather than globally monotonic energy decrease or global symplectic structure.
8. **Budgeters cannot reduce mandatory checks.** Adding predictive computation at high risk may crowd out action-processing deadlines. Reserve minimum checks and degradation paths; terminate candidates when prediction times out.

## 7. Current Frontier Problems and Coverage Boundaries <a href="#section-007" id="section-007"></a>

| Frontier problem | Proposed TC mechanism | What the first round can validate | What the first round cannot promise |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cross-hand/low-data transfer | Embodiment graphs, task-effect interfaces, target-hand decoding | Complete held-out hand types and adaptation curves | Arbitrary hands usable immediately zero-shot |
| Contact and long-horizon errors | Multirate states, contact prediction, observation correction | Fixed short-horizon errors and slip recovery | Success on arbitrarily long sequences |
| Inference cost | Typed residuals and budget selection | Latency/memory/energy at equal quality | Theoretical linearity implying actual speed |
| Verifiable execution | GLS typed errors and action gates | Rejection, degradation, and replay within declared domains | High-dimensional global physical safety |
| Data scarcity | Pretrained representations, human demonstrations, limited target-hand adaptation | Human and robot data costs counted separately | Synthetic data equivalent to real contact ground truth |
| Continuous adaptation | State estimation + window-level bounded parameter/structure updates + generational consolidation | Less drift without more regressions | Unlimited self-evolution or automatic AGI |
| Scientific computing | Similar graph/temporal structures with replaced physical heads and constraints | Independent experiments in one controlled PDE/particle domain | Robot weights directly enabling neuroscience discoveries |
