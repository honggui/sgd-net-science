## 1. Conclusion: Extend into a Model Family, Share Mechanisms and Interfaces, and Decide Weight Sharing by Task <a href="#section-001" id="section-001"></a>

SGD-Net already includes SSMs, graph propagation, dynamic trees, error feedback, and structural projection; documents 07 and 08 already propose SGD-Fold, SGD-Design, SGD-Science-Core, and SGD-Scientist. The largest current gaps are domain-state definitions, trainable objectives, credible data, constraint applicability, and independent validation. Adding names cannot fill these gaps.

Retain **SGD-Science-Core** as a shared mechanism library and **SGD-Net-TC** as the physical-interaction research branch, adding domain configurations for molecules, cells, materials, and neural time series. Sharing begins with data protocols, temporal updates, graph operations, error records, and budget scheduling; transfer experiments must determine weight sharing. Training a unified foundation model covering every domain from scratch is not recommended now.

Here, a world model is a conditional state-evolution predictor. Physical AI adds estimation, planning, control, and execution; AI pharmaceuticals is a multistage development process from candidates to functional validation; AI biology involves systems and interventions at different scales; materials research also includes periodic structures, processing, and characterization. Their scopes differ and cannot be covered by one video-prediction objective.

## 2. Ten Required Extensions <a href="#section-002" id="section-002"></a>

| Number | Extension | Model capabilities or interfaces to add | First acceptance check |
|---|---|---|---|
| E01 | Typed scientific states | Entities, physical variables, units, coordinate frames, samples/embodiments, measurement times, conditions, and sources; separate entity, hypothesis, and execution graphs | Detect unit/identity mismatches; do not interpret missing inputs as zero |
| E02 | Multimodal observation models | Specialized encoders and masks for images, sequences, spectra, omics, touch, etc.; entity binding | Missing-modality and cross-batch tests with strong unimodal baselines |
| E03 | Probabilistic states and multiple time scales | Conditional transitions, observation likelihoods, asynchronous updates, switched/continuous dynamics, multistep rollouts | Prediction-distribution calibration and long-term drift, not just one-step MSE |
| E04 | Domain physical/biological structure | Equivariance, periodic boundaries, energy/forces, reaction stoichiometry, dissipation, or event constraints, selected by domain | Numerical consistency and independent reference calculations, not training-constraint losses alone |
| E05 | Multiscale state migration | Atom–residue, mesh-hierarchy, cell–tissue, and other mappings with readout consistency | Physical-variable error/cost curves after refinement; not all scales must be invertible |
| E06 | Intervention and domain transfer | Intervention types, targets, exposure times, environments, species/cell/embodiment conditions; explicit missingness and confounding assumptions | Genuine held-out interventions/domains; evaluate predictive associations separately from causal effects |
| E07 | Conditional generation and inverse design | Target properties, feasible domains, candidate diversity, costs, multiobjective ranking | Independent validators and prospective candidate evaluation; check surrogate exploitation |
| E08 | Multifidelity errors and budgets | Distinguish insufficient computation, insufficient observations, model bias, random noise; select refinement, more observations, or tools | Outperform fixed-budget/random/rule-based policies at equal total cost |
| E09 | Experimental memory and evidence loops | Raw-data references, versions, batches, negative results, replicates, blinded tests, device feedback | Replayable complete workflows; compare efficiency at comparable experimental quality |
| E10 | Domain adaptation and delivery | Domain-specific heads/adapters, calibration sets, data licenses, model cards, frozen tests, deployment traces | Multiseed results, out-of-domain degradation curves, resource measurements, reproducible experiments |

E01–E03 and E08–E10 are shared foundations; mathematical forms and data for E04–E07 vary by domain. Not every application needs every module: small-sample experimental ranking may start with Gaussian processes and rule systems without deploying the entire neural architecture.

### 2.1 Unified State Interfaces Do Not Mean Unified Physical Semantics <a href="#section-003" id="section-003"></a>

Represent an observation as

$$
o_k=(\mathrm{entity},\mathrm{modality},t_k,y_k,\mathrm{unit},\mathrm{mask},\mathrm{domain},\mathrm{source}).
$$

Domain context includes embodiment parameters or sample conditions; control quantities are $$u$$ and environmental quantities $$c$$. The basic prediction interface is

$$
p_{\theta,d}(x_{t+\Delta},o_{t+\Delta}\mid o_{\leq t},u_{t:t+\Delta},c,d),
$$

where $$d$$ is the domain configuration and $$x$$ the task-required state, not necessarily fully observable ground truth. This is a modeling objective, not a claim that conditional data identify intervention effects. The model must learn parameters through training and be paired with observation-noise models.

Separate biological-sample measurement times, generative denoising steps, numerical-solver iterations, and experimental batch order. Cross-sectional single-cell data, even with pseudotime, cannot directly serve as real longitudinal trajectories of the same cells; sequence position is likewise not physical time.

### 2.2 Five Key Statements in Earlier Designs Requiring Calibration <a href="#section-004" id="section-004"></a>

1. SSM state-change magnitudes are only heuristic signals; use observable innovations, reference-solution differences, or validated error estimates, without inserting unknown future ground truth into online residuals.
2. Denoising is the computational path of a generation algorithm, not inherently molecular dynamics; imposing dissipative constraints does not automatically confer real-dynamics semantics.
3. Spectral radii, state norms, and candidate Lyapunov functions require applicability conditions; alone they cannot guarantee cross-task physical correctness, absence of forgetting in online learning, or valid scientific conclusions.
4. Projection and local regeneration change sample distributions and may damage global structures; test global consistency and distribution bias rather than reporting only fewer local clashes.
5. Separate scientific-hypothesis evidence status from device-action execution permission. Runtime approval of an action does not validate efficacy or mechanisms; database rollback cannot undo real experiments.

## 3. World Models and Physical AI: Add Probabilistic Dynamics and Solver Coordination to TC <a href="#section-005" id="section-005"></a>

SGD-Net-TC already proposes three graphs, contact prediction, memory migration, and embodiment-conditioned actions. Further extension requires moving from point predictions to calibrated future distributions; maintaining uncertainty for occluded and unobserved states; handling multistep errors, contact switching, and external disturbances; delegating computable physics to suitable solvers while using neural modules for unknown or correction terms.

First distinguish two model paths: manipulation uses short-horizon contact/state models and local control; continuous-field tasks use grids/particles or neural operators with boundary conditions. NeuralGCM already demonstrates combining dynamical cores and learned modules, making hybrid approaches worthwhile comparisons, but its climate results do not support direct extrapolation to robotics. [^S01]

Training must check one-step, open-loop multistep, and closed-loop performance together. Low teacher-forced errors may hide rollout failures; planners may actively find model flaws. Evaluate candidates in independent environments and record model rejection, fallback, and task failure. Reserve observation-alignment, checking, submission, and failure-handling budgets within control deadlines.

For known conservative-force components, define scalar potential energy $$E_\theta$$ and derive forces from $$F=-\nabla_x E_\theta$$; model friction, driving, heat baths, collisions, and other terms separately. Do not force all real systems to conserve energy or substitute small position errors for accurate contact forces.

Continue with TC cross-hand short-horizon manipulation as the first task. Continuous media, long-horizon open worlds, and general semantic planning are separate additions, outside first-version acceptance conditions.

## 4. AI Pharmaceuticals: From Structure Enhancement to a Candidate–Function–Experiment Chain <a href="#section-006" id="section-006"></a>

Near-term positioning should be as an enhancer and candidate evaluator for existing models. AlphaFold 3 targets biomolecular interaction structure prediction; official Boltz-2 artifacts include structure- and affinity-related capabilities. These suit integration sources and baselines rather than rebuilding every representation from scratch in SGD-Net. [^S02][^S03]

| Subtask | Required additions | Trainable outputs and validation |
|---|---|---|
| Molecule and complex representation | Atom/residue types, bond orders, stereochemistry, protonation/charge, conformations, environmental conditions | Geometry, interfaces, conformational quality; isolation by protein family/sequence similarity and ligand scaffold |
| Dynamics and interactions | Conformational ensembles, energy/forces or physical-tool interfaces, multifidelity errors | Independent structure/simulation/experiment comparisons; sampling steps are not physical time |
| Candidate ranking | Separate task-defined heads for affinity, selectivity, function, developability, etc. | Endpoint-specific training/calibration; structural confidence is not an efficacy label |
| Inverse design | Constrained candidate generation, multiobjective optimization, candidate diversity | Novelty, out-of-domain bias, and independent experimental performance checks |
| Experimental updates | Assays, experimental systems, batches, quantitative units, negative outcomes, detection limits | Held-out batches/time and prospective validation; handle censored measurements separately |

Small-molecule affinity models cannot become antibody-function evaluators without validation. Binding, receptor activation, cellular phenotypes, in vivo exposure, and disease benefits are distinct endpoints requiring different data. Pharmacokinetics, toxicity, and immune-related properties need dedicated models or experimental interfaces, not merely a larger shared scoring head.

The initial version should select “structural/functional candidate ranking and uncertainty assessment in a fixed task domain,” determining whether it improves on mature models plus simple reranking. Including all ADMET, entirely new protein generation, and full clinical extrapolation would dilute validation focus.

## 5. AI Biology: From Graph Associations to Conditional Perturbations and Population Distributions <a href="#section-007" id="section-007"></a>

Establish a separate cell/tissue configuration: inputs are cell types, genotypes, experimental conditions, omics counts, spatial neighborhoods, and morphology; outputs are conditional phenotypes or expression distributions. Choose count, continuous, or event likelihoods according to measurement processes; do not assume all data are Gaussian or every zero is missing.

scGPT studies single-cell multiomics representations and perturbation prediction. An independent benchmark also reports that complex methods did not outperform simple linear baselines on its evaluated gene-perturbation tasks. Therefore SGD-Net graphs and memory must demonstrate gains; parameter scale or visualized clusters cannot replace evidence. [^S04][^S05]

Particular additions are needed:

- **Conditional intervention representations:** Distinguish targets, types, times, environments, and measurement endpoints; unseen interventions are not randomly split frames of known interventions.
- **Separation of batch and biological signals:** Model donors, batches, platforms, and treatments separately; batch removal must not also erase real treatment effects.
- **Population-distribution prediction:** Single-cell measurements are often destructive, so predict populations under matched conditions rather than inventing paired futures for the same cell.
- **Multiscale mappings:** Cell-to-tissue mappings retain only task-required statistics and conditions; cross-species correspondence is a mapping to test, not direct equivalence of identically named genes.
- **Causal-evidence interfaces:** Store observational associations, mechanistic priors, intervention outcomes, and conflicts separately; counterfactual inference must declare identification assumptions.

Hold out donors, batches, interventions, and combinations, reporting changes relative to controls, differential expression, and functional endpoints. Overall expression correlation may mainly reflect shared background and cannot alone establish intervention prediction. Complete “virtual cells” are a long-term goal; near-term commitments are limited-system perturbation-prediction experiments.

## 6. AI Materials: Periodic Geometry, Energy/Forces, Processing, and Multifidelity Validation <a href="#section-008" id="section-008"></a>

Materials configurations add lattice matrices, fractional coordinates, element occupancy, periodic images, defects, and temperature/pressure conditions to states. Neighbor edges must carry periodic translations; define relationships among positions, cell changes, and strain. Molecular geometry modules cannot serve periodic crystals merely by switching data files.

MatterGen provides a direct conditional-material-generation baseline; UMA provides a family of atomic-system prediction models and artifacts. They represent generation and atomic-property computation, respectively; success in either is not completed materials discovery. [^S06][^S07]

Use a staged path: “candidate generator → SGD state/graph enhancement → inexpensive potential → selected high-fidelity calculations → characterization/experiments.” Extensions include consistent energy/force/stress objectives; crystal symmetry and periodic constraints; composition/structure out-of-domain detection; cross-fidelity bias models; and conditional structure–processing–performance relations.

Low zero-temperature structural energy does not imply thermodynamic stability under specified conditions, much less synthesizability or target functionality. Phase stability requires consistent reference phase sets; kinetics, defects, finite temperatures, and processing may alter outcomes. Define band gaps, ion transport, catalytic activity, and other endpoints separately.

Select one composition family and one explicit property initially, comparing existing potentials/generators plus active screening against SGD enhancement. Separate data by composition family, prototype, and time, preventing small perturbations of the same material from entering both splits. Evaluate property errors, candidate pass rates, DFT call counts, and experimental reproducibility together.

## 7. Neurobehavioral Research: A Multirate Chemical-Transmission–Neural-State–Behavior Model <a href="#section-009" id="section-009"></a>

The project already has a researcher-fact calibration draft (not included) and implementation recommendations (not included). The public cCCTomics paper explicitly covers conditional chemical-transmission tools and rhythmic behavior, supporting this section's task direction; this is not a collaboration request already made by a professor's team. [^S08]

The first layer connects pose/identity models, behavioral events, and neural-signal encoders. The second uses SSMs for real entity time series and graphs for animal interactions, cell types, or chemical-transmission priors. The third predicts behavioral transitions, neural–behavioral relationships, and changes following candidate interventions. **Animal-interaction graphs and chemical-transmission graphs concern different scales** and need intermediate observations and supervision; combining them into one graph does not yield molecule-to-behavior mechanisms.

Required new fields include animal-identity probabilities, experimental day, genetic background, circadian phase, neural-recording quality, intervention events, and synchronization errors. Missing modalities may define independent tasks; do not assume every dataset is paired.

Prioritize “offline prediction of multianimal behavior/rhythms and neural signals, cross-day evaluation, and shadow triggers.” Keep pose models replaceable; compare neural time series with CEBRA, simple state-space/HMM, and linear models. CEBRA joint representations are a relevant precedent, not causal proof. [^S09]

Primary metrics are identity-switch rates, behavioral-event precision/recall, cross-animal/day prediction, calibration, and simulated-trigger false alarms. Only next study predictive value in existing intervention data. Real stimulation requires corresponding platforms and experimental protocols; this document specifies no stimulation parameters or completed biological-mechanism validation.

## 8. Signaling–Synapse Research: A Hierarchical Molecular-Processing–Signaling–Synapse–Disease-Phenotype Model <a href="#section-010" id="section-010"></a>

Research on conversion of proBDNF to mature BDNF and APP brain organoids supplies direct background for processing, synaptic, and disease-phenotype tasks. It supports concrete task mappings, not direct conversion of protein-structure scores into therapeutic conclusions. [^S10][^S11]

Prioritize a predictor of “experimental conditions → time-dependent functional phenotypes.” Inputs distinguish proBDNF, mature BDNF, receptor/pathway measurements, sample types, treatments, observation durations, and baseline states; outputs are signaling readouts, synaptic/morphological, electrophysiological, or other functional endpoints, separately defined from existing data. Avoid combining measurements of different molecular forms into one total-BDNF feature.

Three model interfaces are needed: structural models provide candidates and structural hypotheses; conditional dynamics models learn signaling–phenotype relations in specified systems; active learners suggest measurement batches within allowed candidate spaces. Compare against simple dose–time response models, mixed-effects models, and Gaussian processes before deciding whether SGD graph memory is needed.

Create and validate separate domains for organoids, animals, and human populations, using hierarchical parameters and task adapters where necessary. Shared phenotype mappings may be studied, but shared latent spaces do not establish successful cross-species causal transfer. Retain temporal, measurement, and system differences among observations such as Aβ/Tau, synapses, and cognition.

The first deliverable follows the existing recommendation: “offline active-learning replay of historical functional experiments.” Retain controls, replicates, negative results, and independent tests, comparing sample efficiency at equal quality. If historical experiments used highly selective sampling, offline replay supports only within-pool comparisons; advantages in unmeasured regions require prospective validation.

This round maps projects more concretely than document 34; what remains unknown is the teams' actual available data, platform configurations, sample scales, and experimental objectives, rather than the broad research directions of relevant researchers.

## 9. Training and Budgets: Do Not Stack Every Loss into the Backbone at Once <a href="#section-011" id="section-011"></a>

Use domain-specific objectives

$$
L_d=L_{\mathrm{observation},d}+\lambda_pL_{\mathrm{prediction},d}
+\lambda_sL_{\mathrm{structure},d}+\lambda_mL_{\mathrm{transfer},d}
+\lambda_cL_{\mathrm{calibration},d},
$$

enabling only terms with labels, definitions, and independent validation. Record dimensions, normalization, and weights; decreasing likelihood and structural losses do not directly equal task benefits. Define intervention, generation, and active-selection objectives separately. Naming loss functions cannot create capabilities for objectives without training data.

First freeze mature encoders and base models and train readouts/adapters; then introduce graph and temporal modules individually; finally validate local refinement and budgeters. Compare fully domain-specific, partially shared, and joint training, reporting negative transfer. Small-sample scientific tasks may begin with non-deep-learning baselines, while the platform still supplies data and evidence value.

Active-selection candidates include “recompute, use a more credible tool, add observations, repeat measurements, pause.” Base selection on expected task benefit and calibrated uncertainty, accounting for computation, queues, samples, and measurement costs. Maximum variance is not always most valuable, and repeated measurements do not necessarily reduce model bias. Verify budgeters' predicted benefits against subsequent outcomes.

## 10. Implementation Order and Hardware Implications <a href="#section-012" id="section-012"></a>

| Phase | Deliverables | Passing conditions |
|---|---|---|
| P0 Data and baselines | Scientific-state protocol; one task card per domain; data sources/licenses; frozen splits | Data available, metrics computable, baselines runnable |
| P1 Single-task enhancement | Fixed-graph/fixed-budget versions; replaceable encoders; domain-specific heads | Reproducible benefits over simple and mature baselines, or clear negative results |
| P2 Core research | Individual ablations of E05 state migration, E06 conditional transfer, E08 multifidelity budgets | Attributable benefits with acceptable added compute/experimental costs |
| P3 Prospective validation | Independent validation on new embodiments, batches, compositions, or interventions | Clear coverage and failure boundaries; partial reproduction of historical-replay benefits |
| P4 Deployment | Actual operator traces, memory/bandwidth/deadline profiles, runtime contracts | Software/hardware optimization preserves scientific and control metrics |

If data are available, prioritize three complementary slices: TC physical tasks, offline neurobehavioral tasks, and signaling–synapse experimental replay. Connect existing molecular and materials models first, selecting one narrow task each after data confirmation. Order depends on data and baseline availability, not simultaneous launch of every research track.

Near-term Aegis-X work should complete execution support for bounded sparse graphs, state migration, multirate timestamps, candidate isolation, and error statistics. Atomic-model energy gradients, large omics tables, 3D microscopy, long trajectories, and DFT tools are different workloads; retain CPU/GPU/scientific-tool collaboration first. Determine data types, SPM, DRAM, and dedicated-unit specifications from actual profiles rather than directly increasing TOPS or adding ASICs because of “AI pharmaceutical support.”

Until results under unified protocols exist, state the project claim as “reusable mechanisms and domain-enhancement paths.” Scientific utility is ultimately measured through credible prediction, independent validation, and experimental efficiency, not module counts.
