## 1. Two Data Types Require Two Models <a href="#section-001" id="section-001"></a>

Cross-sectional/destructive-measurement tasks take control populations, cell types, donors, batches, and intervention conditions as inputs and output post-treatment population distributions. There is no natural temporal pairing of individual cells, so matching row numbers in two datasets must not be treated as before/after states.

With real longitudinal imaging or tracking data, a separate entity-temporal version can take the history of the same trackable entity and output subsequent phenotypes. The two tasks may share encoder designs, but require separate training objectives and state management.

scGPT is a relevant baseline for multiomics and perturbation representations; independent research notes that complex models have yet to outperform linear methods on some perturbation tasks. Therefore, task benefits should determine whether graphs, SSMs, and larger pretrained models are enabled. [scGPT](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-024-02201-0), [Perturbation prediction baseline study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-025-02772-6)

## 2. Components and Domain Units <a href="#section-002" id="section-002"></a>

The cross-sectional version selects B03/B05/B06/B07, C01/C02/C08, D01/D02/D03/D06, E02/E06/E08, F01/F02, and G01–G03/G05/G07/G08; enable B01 when paired morphology is available. C03/C05/D07 serve only real spatial neighborhoods; do not attach physical coordinates to arbitrary gene embeddings. D04/D05 are disabled in the initial version, as is D08.

| Unit | Corresponding basic units | Input → output and limitations |
|---|---|---|
| CELL01 Measurement encoding | B05, A05 | Raw counts/depth/QC → cell representations; retain library size and missingness semantics |
| CELL02 Conditional perturbation encoding | B06 | Intervention type, target, time, conditions → intervention vector; separate tests for unseen targets |
| CELL03 Gene/pathway graph | C01/C02, D02/D03 | Reviewed priors and expression features → pathway context; edges carry sources and confidence |
| CELL04 Control-population aggregation | E08 | Control-cell sets under matched conditions → population context and composition |
| CELL05 Conditional response module | D06, E06 | Control context, cell type, perturbation → post-treatment representations or distribution parameters |
| CELL06 Measurement distribution decoding | E02 | Post-treatment representations → gene-count/phenotype distributions; statistical model depends on the measurement |
| CELL07 Spatial/lineage extension | C03/C05, D07 or D04 | Real spatial or lineage/longitudinal records → spatial interactions or temporal information; configure separately |
| CELL08 Change and functional evaluation | G07, E06 | Predicted/measured populations → differences, functional endpoints, and calibration reports |

Gene graphs, cellular spatial graphs, and lineage graphs have different node identities and edge meanings. Multiscale bridging requires explicit gene-to-cell aggregation and cell-to-tissue statistics, rather than simply placing different objects in one adjacency matrix.

## 3. Minimal Forward Pass and Probabilistic Objective <a href="#section-003" id="section-003"></a>

The initial version fixes a gene panel determined from training data/priors, for example 2048 genes, and records what is not covered; each condition batch may sample 64 cells, with d=128 and 4 nongeometric graph blocks. These are debugging dimensions, not conditions for biological sufficiency. Partition using a sparse gene graph and measure memory use and batch size empirically.

CELL01 encodes each cell; CELL04 aggregates a matched control population; CELL02 adds the intervention; CELL03/CELL05 model conditional gene/pathway responses; CELL06 outputs measurement distributions for the treated population. Without paired labels, align sets or distributions rather than minimizing before/after cell errors row by row.

One candidate count head is

$$
Y_{cg}\sim\operatorname{NB}(\mu_{cg},\theta_g),\quad
\mu_{cg}=\ell_c\,\operatorname{softmax}_g(f_\theta(z_c,u,c))_g,
$$

where $$\ell_c$$ is the library-size/depth condition and $$\theta_g>0$$. This is only an option for normalized count modeling; predicting absolute RNA content requires separate measurements and modeling and cannot be inferred from fixed-library-size outputs. Data evaluation determines whether zero inflation is used.

Losses may include measurement NLL, population-mean/distribution distances, changes relative to controls, and supported functional endpoints. Similar distribution distances and means do not ensure correct single-cell mechanisms; do not output causal-network certificates without additional identification conditions.

## 4. Training Workflow and Leakage Prevention <a href="#section-004" id="section-004"></a>

First establish control-mean, cell-type-mean, and linear/low-rank perturbation models, then train conditional heads on frozen omics features; subsequently add graph priors, and finally compare pretraining versus no pretraining and domain-specific fine-tuning. Introduce D04/D05 only in the real longitudinal version, updating time by entity rather than treating pseudotime ordering as true trajectories.

Splits separately cover donors, batches, intervention targets, unseen combinations, and cell types. Fit preprocessing, gene-panel selection, normalization, and batch calibration only on training/allowed adaptation sets. Using test treatment outcomes for batch alignment precludes claims of zero-shot prediction.

If a condition appears in only one batch, batch and treatment effects may be inseparable; model complexity cannot repair experimental-design flaws. Report this limitation rather than forcing a definite treatment effect through batch-removal algorithms.

## 5. Evaluation, Ablations, and Further Extensions <a href="#section-005" id="section-005"></a>

Report changes relative to controls, key genes/pathways, differential expression, population distributions, and functional endpoints, with whole-expression correlation listed separately. The latter may be dominated by many unchanged genes. Group confidence intervals by donor/experimental replicate or condition; cell counts cannot directly serve as independent biological replicate counts.

Ablations: no graph, random prior graphs, no condition encoding, removal of batch inputs, frozen features versus fine-tuning, and simple statistics versus the full model. Validate spatial branches on independent sections/samples; graph attention and edge weights do not automatically represent gene regulatory effects.

Extension to tissue models adds cell composition, spatial neighborhoods, tissue measurements, and corresponding labels; extension to protein-level models requires independent measurement bridges and cannot assume protein activity directly from RNA. This can provide an omics submodule for [43 Signaling–Synapse Research Direction](43.md), but does not automatically transfer the entire cell model.

Deliver data schemas, count preprocessing, condition-encoding rules, prior-graph versions, distribution heads, training/calibration, and cross-condition reports. Minimal acceptance requires outperforming simple baselines on a frozen task, with improvements related to intervention-induced changes; otherwise retain simple models and the data feedback loop.

## Lyapunov Configuration Supplement (2026-09-12) <a href="#section-006" id="section-006"></a>

Do not impose a dynamic V by default on cross-sectional distribution prediction; specify errors and domains through L02–L07 only for real longitudinal observers or numerical solves. L01/L08 record applicability, assumptions, and evidence; see [44 Lyapunov Stability in Detail](44.md) for mathematics and unit interfaces. This is a design awaiting implementation, not a stability validation result.

## Mathematical Task Contract and Extension Priorities (2026-09-12) <a href="#section-007" id="section-007"></a>

Distinguish cell-population distribution mappings from real-time trajectories of individual cells; cross-sectional observations do not automatically determine dynamics. Use held-out perturbation/donor splits, differential expression, and distribution metrics; when identification is impossible, prioritize additional observations or revised assumptions.

Use [48 Mathematical Assessment](48.md) to distinguish representational, learning, and numerical solution capabilities; follow [49 Task Cards](49.md) by starting with fixed-structure baselines and then adding individual extensions supported by evidence. There is no need to first simulate the complete computation of other models.
