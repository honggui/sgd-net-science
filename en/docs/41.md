## 1. Separate Property, Dynamics, and Generative Models First <a href="#section-001" id="section-001"></a>

Property models take crystal structures and conditions as inputs and output energy, stress, or specified properties; dynamics models additionally predict evolution in real time; generative models produce structural candidates according to objectives. The three share periodic geometry and element representations, but have different losses, states, and validation requirements.

For the initial version, build narrow-domain enhancement and candidate screening on existing potentials/materials models, first establishing data availability and error sources. MatterGen can serve as a conditional crystal-generation baseline and UMA as an atomic-property model baseline; do not train both models from scratch simultaneously. [MatterGen](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08628-5), [UMA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.23971)

## 2. Components and Domain Units <a href="#section-002" id="section-002"></a>

Enable B03/B06, C01/C02/C03/C05/C06/C07/C08, D01/D02/D03/D06/D07, E05/E08, F01/F02/F08, and H01. Disable D04/D05 in the static potential-energy version; E07 is a generative extension, and F04 initially uses candidate mode. A real atom cannot be directly split into two atoms merely to support a dynamic tree.

| Unit | Basic mapping | Input → output and internal responsibilities |
|---|---|---|
| MAT01 Periodic structure encoding | A02, B03, C06 | Unit cell, fractional coordinates, elements/occupancy → periodic atomic graph |
| MAT02 Periodic neighborhoods | C03/C06/C08 | Crystal and cutoff radius → edges with translated images and envelopes |
| MAT03 Geometric interaction block | C05, D02/D07 | Periodic relative vectors → equivariant/invariant representations |
| MAT04 Energy/property head | E05/E08 | Atomic representations → extensive energy or separate intensive properties |
| MAT05 Force/stress interface | E05, H07 | Differentiable energy → coordinate/strain derivatives, with units and sign conventions |
| MAT06 Structural candidate head | E07 or H01 | Composition/target conditions → unit-cell and atom candidates; an external generator may be used initially |
| MAT07 Multifidelity correction | B06, F02/F03 | Low-fidelity results and computational settings → high-fidelity predictions and bias estimates |
| MAT08 Processing/experimental readout | E06, G08, H02 | Structures and processing records → specified experimental properties/follow-up measurement recommendations |

Enable MAT08 only when corresponding structure–processing–measurement data exist. Zero-temperature structural predictions do not automatically include real-sample defects, phase composition, or processing history.

## 3. Periodic Geometry and Differentiable Energy <a href="#section-003" id="section-003"></a>

Let lattice column vectors form $$L$$ and fractional coordinates be $$s_i$$; then $$x_i=Ls_i$$, and edges are

$$
r_{ij,n}=L(s_j-s_i+n),\qquad n\in\mathbb Z^3.
$$

Edges record i, j, and n; small unit cells may have multiple valid images, so retaining only one arbitrary nearest image is insufficient. Under a global rotation, L and coordinates transform together. Neighbor searches use physical cutoffs, and energies/forces across cutoffs require sufficiently smooth envelopes.

A reference energy decomposition is $$E_\theta=\sum_i\epsilon_\theta(h_i,c)$$, with conservative forces $$F_i=-\partial E_\theta/\partial x_i$$. Define small strain by $$L'=(I+\varepsilon)L$$ while holding fractional coordinates fixed; this document adopts the tension-positive reference stress $$\sigma=(1/V)\partial E/\partial\varepsilon$$. When connecting data sources, verify signs, component ordering, and pressure conventions. Other conventions require adapter conversions.

Retain original coordinates and the automatic differentiation chain; detaching upstream geometry precludes claims of training energy derivatives. Continuity when discrete graph neighborhoods change requires separate checks. D07's internal computational coordinates are a representation pathway and must not be treated as structural relaxation steps without a solver definition.

A static debugging configuration may use N≤256, E≤4096, d=128, and 4 geometry blocks, with batches adjusted to GPU memory; edge overflow must raise the limit or fail explicitly, rather than silently removing neighbors. Force training requires backpropagating through energy gradients and costs more than one forward pass; measure it empirically.

## 4. Training and Generative Extensions <a href="#section-004" id="section-004"></a>

Data records include elements, unit cells, coordinates, energy references, forces, stresses, computational methods/functionals, temperature and pressure, convergence status, and sources. Separate computational settings into domains or introduce explicit fidelity conditions; do not simply concatenate them as consistent ground truth.

The initial objective combines supervised energy, force, and stress errors, with separate weights according to units and valid labels; disable unlabeled terms when predicting only properties. Determine normalization on the training set and split tests by composition family, structural prototype, and time; adjacent configurations from the same trajectory must not cross the split.

Sequence: existing-model baseline → train only property/bias heads → fine-tune geometry blocks where allowed → bounded-domain dynamics checks → actively select high-fidelity points. Force models require evaluation beyond MAE, including relaxation, stable integration, rare configurations, and out-of-domain behavior.

For a generative branch, explicitly define generated variables as element types, unit cells, and fractional coordinates. Define categorical-generation and continuous-coordinate objectives separately; constrain cell volume/validity and validate with independent calculations after deduplication. Local regeneration requires rechecking the entire unit cell and long-range conditions. A model's own stability prediction does not count as discovery of a new material.

A multiscale approach may refine continuous-field grids or coarse-grained computational representations; mappings from atoms to coarse particles must define mass, charge, and task readouts, and round-trip losses constrain only representable information. Disable F05/F06 when such mappings are undefined.

## 5. Evaluation and Actual Deliverables <a href="#section-005" id="section-005"></a>

Baselines must at least include the original external model, simple bias regression, fixed-fidelity screening, and random/space-filling point selection. Ablate periodic features, geometry blocks, fidelity conditions, active selection, and local refinement.

Report property errors, energy/force consistency, stable-simulation failures, out-of-domain errors, computational call counts, and independent candidate pass rates. Evaluate thermodynamic stability under consistent reference phase sets and conditions; validate band gaps, conductivity, and synthesizability separately.

Process optimization should separately connect MAT08 and aggregate statistics by experimental batch and sample. Deliverables include periodic-graph definitions, unit/stress adapters, training configurations, reference computational settings, and candidate deduplication and validation records. Whether Aegis-X acceleration is worthwhile depends on the time shares of neighbor searches, geometric operators, automatic differentiation, and tools.

## Lyapunov Configuration Supplement (2026-09-12) <a href="#section-006" id="section-006"></a>

Choose applicable V/storage functions for relaxation, integrators, or errors; heat baths and external driving require supply conditions, while static property heads do not enforce dynamic decrease. L01/L08 record applicability, assumptions, and evidence; see [44 Lyapunov Stability in Detail](44.md) for mathematics and unit interfaces. This is a design awaiting implementation, not a stability validation result.

## Mathematical Task Contract and Extension Priorities (2026-09-12) <a href="#section-007" id="section-007"></a>

Declare spaces and symmetries separately for periodic cells, structures, and compositions. Evaluate energy, forces, stress, and stability separately; choose norms that control the required derivatives. For prediction across grids/system scales, verify normalization and integration weights; changing atom counts does not directly imply size generalization.

Use [48 Mathematical Assessment](48.md) to distinguish representational, learning, and numerical solution capabilities; follow [49 Task Cards](49.md) by starting with fixed-structure baselines and then adding individual extensions supported by evidence. There is no need to first simulate the complete computation of other models.
