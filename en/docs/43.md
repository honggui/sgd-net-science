## 1. What the First Model Answers <a href="#section-001" id="section-001"></a>

Within a specified experimental system, predict “how sample baselines and recorded treatment conditions affect subsequent signaling and functional phenotypes.” Distinguish proBDNF, mature BDNF, receptor/pathway readouts, synaptic morphology, and functional endpoints rather than collapsing them into a single overall BDNF score.

Relevant studies address proBDNF processing and phenotypes, and disease-related pathology in APP brain organoids. These provide task background; structure prediction cannot replace the corresponding functional measurements. [proBDNF study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41380-024-02595-5), [Brain organoid study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41422-024-00957-w)

The initial S0 version provides conditional regression and active-learning replay on historical sample-level functional experiments; add the S1 temporal model when genuinely longitudinal records exist; only S2 investigates sharing across domains such as animals and organoids. Validate the three separately.

## 2. Component Configuration and Domain Units <a href="#section-002" id="section-002"></a>

S0 selects B03/B04/B06/B07, C01/C02/C08, D01/D02/D03/D06, E02/E06/E08, F01/F02, G01–G03/G05/G07/G08, and H01/H02/H04/H08. Add B01/B05 when imaging/omics are available, respectively; enable D04/D05 only in S1. Disable C05/D07 without real geometry, and do not represent all signaling pathways through molecular coordinates.

| Unit | Basic mapping | Input → output and limitations |
|---|---|---|
| SYN01 Sample/batch encoding | A01/A03/A06/A08 | Samples, well positions, batches, species/systems → identities and conditions; identity keys are not general biological features |
| SYN02 Processing and signaling representation | B03/B04, C01/C02 | Molecular/pathway measurements distinguishing forms → typed nodes and conditional graphs |
| SYN03 Morphology/function encoding | B01/B04/B05 | Microscopy, electrophysiology, omics → modality features and QC; do not force fusion without pairing |
| SYN04 Conditional response/temporal block | B06, D06 or D04/D05 | Baselines, conditions, observation times → prediction representations; separate cross-sectional and longitudinal cases |
| SYN05 Multi-endpoint readout | E02/E06/E08 | Representations → means, scales, or distributions for each functional endpoint |
| SYN06 Cross-system adaptation | B06, G06 | Shared features plus domain parameters → system-specific outputs; retain domain-specific baselines |
| SYN07 Experiment selector | G08, F02/F03 | Known records and candidate pools → next-batch recommendations, including replicate/control costs |
| SYN08 Replay and result tracking | H02/H04/H08 | Selection process and real results → benefit, bias, and failure records |

Structure/molecular models from [39 SGD-Mol](39.md) may provide additional candidate features, and omics may connect to [40 SGD-Cell](40.md). Without paired functional labels, these features only support candidate hypotheses and cannot accomplish end-to-end functional prediction.

## 3. Minimal Data Schema, Tensors, and Forward Pass <a href="#section-003" id="section-003"></a>

Each record includes sample system, baseline, treatment identifier and its reviewed conditions, observation time, endpoint definition/units, detection limits, batch, QC, replicates, and raw-data references. Register repeated before/after measurements separately from same-time measurements of different samples.

S0 may use N≤32 reviewed measurement/pathway nodes, d=128, 2–4 ordinary graph blocks, and sample batch≤32 for debugging. Nodes are variables/measurements, not additional cells. With few records, start with Gaussian processes or mixed-effects models; deep configurations are optional.

The forward pass is sample-condition validation → modality encoders → typed pathway messages → conditional fusion → multi-endpoint distribution heads. S1 updates according to real time for the same trackable entity; destructive measurements support only conditional population prediction. Time labels without pairing cannot train a same-entity trajectory.

SYN02's prior edges retain the types “literature prior/supported by this experiment/to be tested.” Learnable edge weights are predictive parameters, not directly interpretable as reaction rates or causal edges. A mechanistic ODE requires separate definitions of states, reaction terms, units, observation mappings, and identifiable parameters before comparison with data-driven models.

## 4. Training Objectives and Incremental Construction <a href="#section-004" id="section-004"></a>

The initial version uses continuous regression/NLL, classification, or count likelihoods separately by endpoint; censored measurements use interval information, and missing values are not zeros. Model baseline and batch conditions, but report inseparability if treatments and batches are completely confounded; algorithms cannot guarantee confounding removal.

First build constant, baseline-adjusted, conditional-response, mixed-effects, and Gaussian-process baselines. Then fix preprocessing and train SYN04/SYN05; add SYN02 graphs and SYN03 multimodality when information is sufficient. Compare S1 against a version without temporal memory. For S2, compare fully separate domains, a shared backbone with adapters, and a joint model, reporting negative transfer independently.

Losses comprise valid-endpoint NLL, measurable temporal consistency, and justified structural constraints. Do not arbitrarily force all responses to be monotonic: impose monotonicity only on endpoints supported by domain knowledge and validation. Define endpoint weights and experimental reliability in advance; do not change primary metrics after seeing test performance.

Split by batch, donor/animal, experimental system, and time, holding out treatment conditions where necessary. Technical replicates are not independent biological replicates. Separate calibration and test sets, and use only validation sets for model selection.

## 5. Establishing Valid Active-Learning Replay <a href="#section-005" id="section-005"></a>

Start from a fixed initial set within a historically measured candidate pool. Random selection, space-filling/rule-based methods, Gaussian processes, and the SGD selector independently choose the next point, revealing its historical label only after selection. All methods use identical budgets, pools, initial sets, and quality rules, with multiple random seeds.

Retain controls and necessary replicates; budgets include measurement failures and sample costs. Do not use remaining labels to choose the “most promising” initial set in advance. When candidate outcomes are missing, do not substitute the current model's predictions as ground truth to prove its own selection advantage.

Selection objectives may be reducing a prespecified endpoint error, increasing verified candidate hits, or distinguishing competing hypotheses. Information gain/uncertainty only guides selection; its actual value is evaluated using real outcomes after revelation. Historical pools contain selection bias, so offline benefits cannot be extrapolated into unmeasured space; prospective validation is needed later.

## 6. Validation and Scientific Outputs <a href="#section-006" id="section-006"></a>

Report endpoint-specific errors, cross-batch calibration, differences from simple models, degradation under missing modalities, and measurement counts required for equal accuracy. Ablate graph priors, multimodality, temporal memory, domain adaptation, and active selection. Predicted signaling changes do not directly imply synaptic improvement, much less therapeutic benefit for disease.

Use different domains and output definitions across animals, organoids, and human populations. Shared latent representations support only a hypothesis of representation reuse, not proof of causal transfer. For competing explanations of disease mechanisms, record subsequent independent evidence and conflicts rather than outputting a unique “root cause.”

Deliverables include experimental data dictionaries, endpoint/unit protocols, prior-graph sources, domain-specific configurations, training/calibration weights, frozen tests, and active-replay reports. Actual device orchestration follows existing platform protocols; this research document defines no dispensing or biological intervention parameters.

## Lyapunov Configuration Supplement (2026-09-12) <a href="#section-007" id="section-007"></a>

Stability concerns fitting/estimation and well-defined longitudinal errors; it does not presume a unified decreasing potential function for BDNF, synaptic, or disease phenotypes. L01/L08 record applicability, assumptions, and evidence; see [44 Lyapunov Stability in Detail](44.md) for mathematics and unit interfaces. ## Mathematical Task Contract and Extension Priorities (2026-09-12) <a href="#section-008" id="section-008"></a>

Distinguish conditional prediction, dynamics, and intervention responses for signaling, synaptic, and organoid endpoints; explicitly define states and observation mappings for proBDNF, mature BDNF, and others. Define steady-state, transient, and endpoint errors separately; increasing network size alone cannot guarantee recovery of unmeasured hidden states.

Use [48 Mathematical Assessment](48.md) to distinguish representational, learning, and numerical solution capabilities; follow [49 Task Cards](49.md) by starting with fixed-structure baselines and then adding individual extensions supported by evidence. There is no need to first simulate the complete computation of other models.
