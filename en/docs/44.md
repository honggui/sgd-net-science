## 1. Conclusions and Connection to the Original Design <a href="#section-001" id="section-001"></a>

Retain Lyapunov mechanisms and explicitly incorporate them into SGD-Net's model, training, and checking paths. Section 13 of [Document 13](13.md) already contains stability projection and Lyapunov energy checks, and Section 17 contains corresponding losses. The newer version distributes these responsibilities among F01, G01, H04, and others without fully registering V functions, decrease conditions, and correction paths, weakening an important design. L01–L08 complete these here.

The project's existing world-model review draft (not included in this package) also explicitly distinguishes hidden-state stability from physical/safety guarantees. The “Technical White Paper on Stable World Models Driven by a Generalized Lyapunov Paradigm.md” linked in the older draft was not found in this workspace search, so its full text cannot be claimed to have been reverified. This revision relies on readable documents 13, 25, 36, and the review draft.

A layered scheme of “Lyapunov stability + input/dissipation conditions + barrier safety” is recommended. The latter two extend applicability rather than replace Lyapunov methods. Equivariance addresses transformation consistency and Lyapunov methods address stability of specified dynamics; they complement each other. Likewise, stability cannot automatically establish scientific correctness.

## 2. First Declare What Must Be Stable <a href="#section-002" id="section-002"></a>

| Object | Reference quantity and objective | Distinctions to retain |
|---|---|---|
| Internal memory/observer | Difference between two memory trajectories under identical inputs, or labeled estimation error | Driving h to zero does not mean remembering information; new observations can reasonably increase state norms |
| Numerical/inference iterations | Defined residuals, feasibility, or objectives | Refinement k is not physical time k; small residuals may still accompany an incorrect model |
| Physical closed loop | Errors relative to target equilibria or reference trajectories | The original world model should retain real instability, chaos, and external inputs |
| Graph/mode switching | Comparable V values and state mappings before/after switching | Added graph nodes must not imply increased energy solely because more terms are summed |
| Stochastic processes | Conditional expected drift or probability bounds | Finite-sample averages are not proofs of deterministic decrease for every sample |

Stability contracts must contain `scope/state/reference/domain/dynamics/dt/input_set/noise_bound/graph_version/V_version/condition/tolerance/evidence_level`. Without defined fixed points, trajectories, inputs, and regions, report only numerical diagnostics, not established system stability.

## 3. Group L: Eight Stability Units <a href="#section-003" id="section-003"></a>

| ID | Unit | Input → output | Type and execution location |
|---|---|---|---|
| L01 | Stability contract and reference | Task/dynamics/domain → errors, assumptions, and checking mode | D; before the forward pass; mark inapplicable or unknown without definitions |
| L02 | V/storage-function head | Errors, graph, domain → V and gradients where applicable | T/D; alongside prediction heads, independently versioned |
| L03 | Drift/decrease evaluation | Current/candidate states, inputs, V → condition residuals | D; check temporal updates, refinement, and switching separately |
| L04 | Disturbance and supply budget | Input/error bounds → allowed supply and robust margins | D/T; separate estimates from verified upper bounds |
| L05 | State/action correction | Candidates, constraints, budgets → corrections or infeasibility status | D/T; line search, constrained optimization, or verified solvers |
| L06 | Topology-switch compatibility | P/Q or mode resets → V comparability and switching results | D/T; before graph-version switching; connects F05/F06/F08 |
| L07 | Stability training and counterexamples | Data/candidate V → losses and counterexample samples | T/X; connects G01/G03/G07, mainly offline |
| L08 | Evidence and failure handling | Condition residuals, proof/sampling records → status and fallback | D/X; connects H03/H04/H05/H08 |

L01/L08 are general responsibilities; enable other units only in branches with declared stability objectives. Static classification or generation tasks need not forcibly train a Lyapunov head. Prediction models, V networks, correctors, and provers/checkers are distinct artifacts.

## 4. Trainable V and Decrease Objectives <a href="#section-004" id="section-004"></a>

For error e relative to a declared target, one positive-definite parameterized candidate is

$$
V_\psi(e)=e^T(R^TR+\epsilon I)e+
\|g_\psi(e)-g_\psi(0)\|^2,\quad\epsilon>0.
$$

In Euclidean error space this gives V(0)=0 and V>0 for e≠0, but does not automatically ensure decrease along model trajectories. Normalize dimensions according to the contract first; manifold states require valid errors or tangent-space coordinates, rather than unconditionally treating global coordinate differences as geodesic errors.

A candidate discrete-time check is

$$
V(e^+)-V(e)\leq-\alpha\|e\|^2+\sigma(\|w\|),\quad\alpha>0.
$$

Here w denotes allowed disturbances/input differences under the contract, and σ is a specified gain. Decrease without disturbances concerns stability; with inputs, input-to-state stability or ultimate boundedness can be studied. Positive-definite upper/lower bounds, domain invariance, and corresponding regularity conditions are needed to draw conclusions from local inequalities. With variable time intervals, decrease parameters and disturbance budgets must also match Δt.

Training may add the positive-part penalty `[ΔV+α||e||²−σ(||w||)]_+`, but low loss on the training distribution is only empirical evidence. Counterexample search may supplement training; record a conditional proof only when the corresponding verifier completes validation supporting the condition in the declared domain. Search timeout or “no counterexamples found randomly” is not proof. Neural Lyapunov control already has precedents combining learners and counterexample checkers. [Neural Lyapunov Control](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2019/hash/2647c1dba23bc0e0f9cdf75339e120d2-Abstract.html)

If physical prediction-error bounds depend on unobservable true states, runtime must not read them directly; observer bounds are needed, or these quantities must remain offline test measures.

## 5. Connecting SSMs, EGNNs, and Dynamic Trees <a href="#section-005" id="section-005"></a>

**SSM/graph feedback:** Check stability of the complete memory update z⁺=F(z,u,G), not only diagonal A. For two trajectories under the same u, contraction of V(z−z') may be studied, avoiding forced memory return to zero. Exponential decay in 36 limits only some homogeneous terms, not the full Jacobian of graph messages and correction feedback. Contraction analysis is a relevant tool for trajectory stability, but does not guarantee that global contraction suits every task. [Original contraction analysis paper](https://web.mit.edu/nsl/www/preprints/contraction.pdf)

**EGNN:** V is a scalar invariant under the declared group, and corrections should preserve the corresponding equivariance. If geometric error e transforms by Q, a quadratic form with arbitrary P is not automatically rotation-invariant; require QᵀPQ=P or construct V from invariants such as distances and inner products. Declare groups separately for chiral tasks and general manifolds. Euclidean geometric gradient corrections may preserve equivariance only when V is invariant and constraints/step-size rules are compatible; using V alone does not establish it.

**Dynamic graphs:** For coarse/fine graphs G/G' and state migration P_G, check

$$
V_{G'}(P_Gz)\leq V_G(z)+\eta_{switch}.
$$

η_switch is not a tolerance that can be spent indefinitely. Use a common V, multiple-Lyapunov switching/dwell conditions, or an accumulable bounded error budget. Allowing a small increase at each switch may still diverge after unlimited switching. Spectral radii below 1 for each individual matrix also do not guarantee stability of a switched system.

If V sums over nodes, refinement requires mass/volume or other weights and consistent physical readouts to avoid artificial doubling by copying parent states. Failed graph changes may undo uncommitted computational candidates; physical actions that have already occurred cannot be rolled back.

**High dimensions and manifolds:** Lyapunov methods are not limited to three dimensions. Applicability depends on state spaces, dynamics, and V conditions, not vector dimension. Non-Euclidean, multimetric, and quasimetric choices in D08 cannot directly inherit Euclidean proofs through arbitrary norm substitution.

## 6. Physical Control: Distinct Roles of CLF, CBF, and Dissipation <a href="#section-006" id="section-006"></a>

For a known control-affine model ẋ=f(x)+g(x)u, use a control Lyapunov function V for target errors and a barrier function b for the allowed domain. Under actuator constraints, one may solve

$$
\min_{u,\delta\geq0}\|u-u_{nom}\|_R^2+p\delta^2,
$$
$$
L_fV+L_gV\,u\leq-\alpha V+\delta,\qquad
L_fb+L_gb\,u\geq-\kappa b,\qquad u\in\mathcal U.
$$

This is only a CLF/CBF reference interface when control-affine, relevant relative-degree, and other conditions hold. Relaxation through δ removes strict decrease guarantees; CBFs, input saturation, and model errors may cause infeasibility. Solve failure, latency, sample-and-hold behavior, and disturbances require contracts and independent handling; continuous-time conditions cannot directly serve as discrete hardware guarantees. Joint CLF/CBF optimization already has original research precedents. [Ames et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.06408)

For systems with external driving, friction, or heat exchange, a storage function satisfying ΔV≤supply−dissipation is more appropriate than requiring raw physical energy to decrease at every step. Potential energy may increase when a robot lifts an object; oscillatory or chaotic world models also must not be forced to converge to rest. Where needed, stabilize tracking errors, numerical errors, or estimators rather than eliminating real phenomena.

## 7. Enabling the Mechanisms in Seven Domains <a href="#section-007" id="section-007"></a>

| Domain | Recommended check objects | Default constraints not adopted |
|---|---|---|
| World models | Memory input responses, estimation errors, numerical rollout errors; bounds only with proof conditions | Do not force real-world states toward zero or all trajectories toward each other |
| Physical AI/dexterous hands | Local closed-loop tracking CLFs, allowed-domain CBFs, contact-mode switching, input budgets | Do not treat prediction confidence as safety bounds or ignore actuators and impacts |
| Pharmaceuticals/molecules | Objective decrease in specified structural optimization, stability of applicable simulators | Denoising paths are not physical dynamics; molecular energy is not efficacy |
| Biology/cells | Numerical solves, errors/boundedness of observable longitudinal models | Do not force expression, cell cycles, or population responses to decrease monotonically |
| Materials | Relaxation objectives, numerical properties of integrators and declared thermodynamic ensembles | No per-step energy decrease requirement under heat baths/driving; static property heads need no dynamic V |
| Neurobehavioral research | Numerical stability of observers/neural time series, shadow-trigger execution conditions | Do not erase rhythms or behavioral transitions, or infer neural mechanisms from stability |
| Signaling–synapse research | Estimation/fitting processes and errors with longitudinal conditions; experimental-device boundaries | Do not presume universal decreasing potentials for signaling, synaptic, or disease phenotypes |

## 8. Implementation and Acceptance <a href="#section-008" id="section-008"></a>

First validate V computation, positive-definite parameterization, decrease, and infeasibility paths using fixed graphs, low dimensions, and known dynamics; introduce external inputs and check misclassification of normal inputs as anomalies; then test graph switching, noise, delays, and incorrect error bounds. Only afterward train neural V, separating small-domain formal verification from large-domain statistical evaluation.

Ablations separately compare no stability term, spectral/norm constraints only, V training regularization, added correctors, and added domain verification. Report task accuracy, violation rates, correction magnitudes, false rejection, infeasibility rates, and total cost. Prevent “reject everything” or “predict everything stationary” from creating spurious stability performance.

With these eight registered units, the overall architecture now consists of the original eight groups of 64 units plus group L's eight units, totaling nine groups and 72 units; the original 64 IDs remain, and domain-unit counts are unchanged. This remains a count of design components, not implemented neural layers.
