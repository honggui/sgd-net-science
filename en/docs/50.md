## 1. Assessment and Existing Foundations <a href="#section-001" id="section-001"></a>

SGD-Net has design foundations supporting reflection, review, and retrospective analysis, but has not yet demonstrated reliable attribution or continuous improvement in actual task performance. This direction connects memory, world models, error analysis, and continual learning; generating reflective text or increasing branch counts is not a success criterion.

[17 Original Retrospective Design](17.md) already proposed experience replay, counterfactual sandboxes, metacognitive monitoring, and long-term consolidation; [36 Overall Architecture](36.md) retains interfaces including F/G/H; [46 Continuous Evolution](46.md) defines prediction snapshots, error diagnosis, candidate training, and persistent acceptance. Existing interfaces do not mean that error-cause identification has been implemented.

## 2. Three Capabilities and Six Foundations <a href="#section-002" id="section-002"></a>

| Capability | Core question | Necessary mechanisms |
|---|---|---|
| Review | What happened then, and on what basis were choices made? | Retrievable experiences, event order, pre-observation predictions and versions |
| Retrospective analysis | Which decisions affected outcomes, and what might alternatives have produced? | Trajectory replay, evaluation, causal hypotheses, and alternative testing |
| Reflection | Do assumptions, strategies, or learning methods need to change? | Self-capability assessment, attribution, revision, and subsequent validation |

The engineering foundations are experience memory, evaluation criteria, error attribution, alternatives, validation mechanisms, and experience consolidation. These are operational engineering definitions, not a complete theory of human cognition or claims of subjective consciousness in the model.

Reflection may combine structured states, numerical computation, and language. Language aids description and retrieval, but fluent explanations do not prove causes correct. Compressed SSM states also do not guarantee experience recovery; key events require separate traceable records.

## 3. Connecting Interfaces into an Inspectable Closed Loop <a href="#section-003" id="section-003"></a>

| Stage | Existing attachment points | Contracts to complete |
|---|---|---|
| Event memory | F08, G05, H08, EV01 | Event/entity IDs, observations and missingness masks available then, predictions, actions, alternatives, time, versions, sources, cost; align outcomes separately when they arrive |
| Evaluation and case selection | F01/F02/F03, EV02 | Task loss, data quality, recurrence frequency, and expected retrospective benefit; both successes and failures may be selected |
| Candidate causes | EV02, FN04/FN05 | Distinguish observation, representation, learning, solve, and prior mismatches; record supporting/refuting evidence and allow unknowns |
| Alternatives | E01, F04/F08, H01 | Isolated replay and candidate states; register assumptions, action changes, applicable scope, and model uncertainty |
| Revision and competition | EV03–EV06, G05/G06/G07 | Compare no change, memory updates, strategy changes, local training, solve changes, additional observations, and necessary growth |
| Acceptance and consolidation | EV07/EV08, H08, L06/L08 | Benefits on new evidence, degradation of old capabilities, costs, and applicable stability checks; versioned retention, reversal, and distillation |

These refine responsibilities of existing units; they do not additionally claim six implemented networks. Event records preserve information available at the time; later labels cannot be backfilled as if already known. Label real outcomes, simulation outputs, and model-generated hypotheses separately.

Detecting an error is not identifying its cause. EV02 must supply testable candidate causes and observations/tests distinguishing them, rather than only an unverified causal label. The model's own explanation is candidate evidence; multiple answers from the same model do not automatically provide independent validation.

## 4. Connecting Function-Space and Numerical Error Analysis <a href="#section-004" id="section-004"></a>

Write the output as `y_hat = f_{theta,A}(x;m)`, where theta denotes parameters, A structure, and m memory. The outer reflection process is:

```text
Experiences and evidence → Candidate causes → Select memory/parameter/structure/solve/observation adjustments
  → Test on independent or subsequent data → Accept, retain unknown status, or roll back
```

Ordinary parameter training primarily searches for a better function within an established representation family. Deeper reflection also checks whether that family, input information, constraints, and evaluation metrics suit the task, following the error distinctions in [48 Mathematical Assessment](48.md). Reference solutions, actual outcomes, checkable constraints, and subsequent independent data provide evidence of differing strength; improvement cannot be declared solely through model self-evaluation.

Consider widening or new experts only for insufficient representation; prioritize information for insufficient observations; consider tolerances, precision, preconditioning, and iterations for inadequate solves; revise problematic model assumptions when assumptions are wrong. Changes to evaluation metrics must be separately registered and comparable baselines rebuilt; lowering standards must not manufacture benefits. Check Lyapunov conditions only for declared objects and applicable domains, without requiring all dynamics to converge monotonically.

## 5. Dexterous-Hand Retrospective Example and Counterfactual Boundaries <a href="#section-005" id="section-005"></a>

During a grasp, the pre-observation prediction says the object will not slip, but subsequent real tactile and pose observations show slipping. Review preserves predictions, tactile delays, contact estimates, actions, and model versions. Retrospective analysis proposes competing hypotheses such as erroneous friction estimates, tactile delays, contact-mode misclassification, or inappropriate actions, distinguishing them through records, controlled tests, or simulations with credible validity ranges.

If evidence points to friction estimation, update that estimate first; if sensor delays are responsible, address temporal alignment; do not always add experts. A world model can compare alternative actions, but its simulations hold only under its assumptions and do not directly prove real counterfactuals. Replaying correlated trajectories also does not automatically identify causal effects.

Finally, test in subsequent grasps whether similar failures decrease, successes are reproducible, and performance on old objects degrades. The same boundary applies to pharmaceuticals, biology, and related research: conditional associations and simulated interventions cannot replace evidence from real interventions.

## 6. Training and Acceptance Recommendations <a href="#section-006" id="section-006"></a>

First complete recording and replay, connect task evaluation, then validate candidate causes and bounded revisions, and only finally consider persistent structural changes, consistent with [49 Capability-First Roadmap](49.md).

Controls must at least distinguish fixed models, added memory, repeated inference under equal budgets, retrospective analysis with evaluation, and retrospective analysis plus persistent learning. Control total computation, data, and tuning budgets to avoid mistaking greater compute expenditure for benefits from reflection mechanisms.

Metrics include subsequent independent-task benefits, recurrence of similar errors, the fraction of correct results changed to incorrect ones, error detection and confidence calibration, old-task forgetting, retrospective latency, and total cost. Evaluate causal-identification accuracy only on controlled tasks with verifiable causes; real complex cases may remain unknown. Reflection needs triggering and stopping conditions; repetitive low-value retrospective analysis also consumes resources.

Isolate test sets from replay training data; continuous version selection erodes validation-set validity, so use subsequent time periods or refreshed evaluation data. No thresholds claimed to be optimal have currently been set, and these experiments have not been performed.

## 7. Literature Basis and Limitations <a href="#section-007" id="section-007"></a>

- [Reflexion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11366): improves subsequent attempts through feedback and experience memory, potentially without updating model weights; it is not evidence that SGD-Net already has equivalent capabilities.
- [Large Language Models Cannot Self-Correct Reasoning Yet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01798): in its studied settings, intrinsic self-correction without external feedback has limitations and may change correct answers to incorrect ones; this must not be generalized into a claim that all models can never self-correct.
- [SCoRe](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.12917): improves self-correction in the tested language models through dedicated reinforcement-learning training; it does not imply equal benefits for arbitrary architectures or scientific tasks.

These sources were verified in the previous discussion round; this round records the discussion. Together they show that feedback, memory, training methods, and evaluation conditions require concrete design. The project's candidate contribution is choosing improvement actions from credible error evidence and demonstrating subsequent capability gains, rather than the concept of reflection itself.
